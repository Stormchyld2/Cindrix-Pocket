People die of the wrong problem first. A simple rule keeps the order straight: you can last about **3 minutes** without air or in deep panic, about **3 hours** in harsh cold or heat without shelter, about **3 days** without water, and about **3 weeks** without food.

## What that means
- **Danger first.** Get away from fire, flood, falling trees or a road before anything else.
- **Warmth and shelter next.** Cold and wet kill faster than hunger. Most outdoor deaths are from cold exposure, not starvation.
- **Water third.** You can still think and walk on day two without water, but by day three you are in trouble.
- **Food last.** A hungry person who is warm, hydrated and calm is fine for weeks. Food becomes the main job only in a long stay.

## Spend energy like money
Sweat wets your clothes and dehydrates you. Work slowly, in the cool of the day, and rest before you are tired. Every job you do should answer one question: does it fix the next thing that will hurt me?

## Keep your head
Fear makes people run, shed warm clothes and skip steps. Say your plan aloud: "Danger, warmth, water, signal, then food." Saying it keeps you steady.
