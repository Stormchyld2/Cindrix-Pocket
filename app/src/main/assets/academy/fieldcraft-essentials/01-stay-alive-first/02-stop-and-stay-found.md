Most lost people are found within a day or two if someone knows where to look. The mistake that turns a bad day into a disaster is walking farther and farther from the last place anyone knows you were.

## S.T.O.P.
1. **Stop.** Sit down. Take a drink if you have one. Do not move for five minutes.
2. **Think.** When did you last know where you were? What do you have? How much daylight is left?
3. **Observe.** Look at the weather, the ground, the water, your body, and what you carry.
4. **Plan.** Pick one plan and say it aloud.

## Stay or go?
**Stay** if someone knows your route, you are hurt, it is getting dark, the weather is bad, or you are near where you were last seen. Make yourself easy to find: a bright cloth, a clear spot, a signal fire ready to light.

**Go** only if you know exactly where help is, your group is in danger where it is, or no one will miss you for days. If you go, leave a note with your name, the time, and your direction, and mark your path.

## Tell someone before you go
Every trip: where, route, when you will be back, who to call if you are late. This is the best survival tool you own, and it weighs nothing.

## Do it
- [ ] Write a trip note for your next outing: where, route, return time, and who to call.
- [ ] Say the S.T.O.P. steps from memory to another person.

## Check yourself

? What is the first thing to do when you realise you are lost?
- Run to higher ground
- Walk faster to get out before dark
+ Stop and sit still for a few minutes
- Shout for ten minutes
> Stopping prevents the panic that makes things worse.

? When should you usually stay put?
- When you are bored
- When you can see a road you do not know
- Never
+ When someone knows your route or you are hurt
> People who know your plan will look where you are.

? What is the best survival tool for any trip?
- A big knife
- A thick coat only
- A map you never read
+ Telling someone your route and return time
> Someone knowing where you are gets you found.
