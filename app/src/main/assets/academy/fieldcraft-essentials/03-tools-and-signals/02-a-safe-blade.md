A sharp tool is safer than a dull one, because you push less. Most field injuries come from the hand holding the work, not the hand holding the blade.

## Rules
- **Cut away from your body.** Make a blood circle: stretch your arm out and spin; nobody and nothing should be inside it.
- Cut on a stump or log, never with the work held in your other hand against your leg.
- **Carry closed** or sheathed. Pass a knife handle first.
- Keep it sharp and dry.
- Do not baton a thin knife or a folding knife. Use a fixed blade, or a saw.

## If you cut yourself
Apply direct pressure with a clean cloth for 10 minutes. See the Academy courses on bleeding and wound care for deep cuts.

## Without a knife
A sharp-edged stone, a broken bottle edge (handled with great care), or the edge of a tin can will cut cord, bark and food.
