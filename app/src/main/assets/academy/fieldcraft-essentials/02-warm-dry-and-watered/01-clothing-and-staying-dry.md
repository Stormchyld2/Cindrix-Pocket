Wet clothes pull body heat away about twenty-five times faster than dry air does. Staying dry matters more than staying covered.

## The three layers
- **Base:** wool or synthetic next to the skin. Not cotton, which holds water.
- **Middle:** fleece or wool for warmth.
- **Outer:** a shell that stops wind and rain.

## Rules that work
- **Add or remove a layer before you sweat or shiver**, not after.
- Keep your head, neck, hands and feet covered. A lot of heat leaves there.
- Keep a dry set of socks and a hat in a waterproof bag.
- **Wet feet** end trips. Dry your socks against your body and change them whenever you stop.

## With nothing extra
- A trash bag becomes a rain poncho: cut holes for your head and arms.
- Stuff dry leaves, grass or newspaper inside your jacket for warmth. It must stay dry.
- Never sit or lie directly on cold ground. Put branches, bark or a pack under you.

## Warning signs of cold injury
Shivering, clumsy hands, slurred speech, and confusion mean the body is losing the fight. Get out of the wind, remove wet clothing, add dry layers, warm sweet drinks if the person can swallow, and share body warmth. Call for help if you can.

## Check yourself

? Which clothing to avoid in cold, wet weather?
- Wool
- Fleece
- A rain shell
+ Cotton
> Cotton stays wet and keeps pulling heat away.

? When should you add or remove a layer?
- After you are soaked
+ Before you sweat or shiver
- Only at camp
- Never
> Sweat soaks the layers and chills you later.

? A person is shivering hard and slurring words. What is the first move?
- Rub them hard with snow
- Make them run
+ Get them out of the wind and wet, then add dry layers
- Give them alcohol
> Stop heat loss first. Rubbing and alcohol make things worse.
