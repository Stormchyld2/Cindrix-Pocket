Clear water can still make you sick. Treat every wild water source, even a spring.

## Finding it
- Look **downhill**, at the bottom of valleys, and where green plants grow thickly.
- Follow animal trails at dawn and dusk. Birds head to water in the evening.
- **Dig** a hole in the outer bend of a dry streambed and let it seep.
- **Rain and dew:** catch rain in anything that holds water. Wipe dew off grass with a cloth in the early morning and wring it out.
- **Snow and ice** need heat to melt. Melting them in your mouth chills you.
- Choose moving water over still water, and upstream of camps, animals and roads.

## Making it safe
1. **Strain** through a cloth to remove dirt.
2. **Boil** at a rolling boil for **1 minute** (3 minutes above 6,500 feet). This is the most reliable method.
3. **No pot?** Fill a clear plastic bottle, lay it in bright sun for **6 hours** (or 2 days if cloudy). This kills many germs, but boiling is better.

## Do not
- Drink seawater, urine or blood.
- Eat snow in cold weather without melting it first.
- Drink from water with a chemical smell, an oily sheen, or dead animals nearby. Treatment will not remove poisons.

## Do it
- [ ] Find a water source on a local map, then walk to it and treat a cup of it by boiling.
- [ ] Make a strainer from a cloth and a funnel from a bottle.

+ Moving water upstream of camps and animals
- A still pond with algae
- Puddles on a road
- Water with an oily sheen
> Moving water is usually cleaner.

- Yes in small amounts
- Yes after boiling
+ No, it dehydrates you further
