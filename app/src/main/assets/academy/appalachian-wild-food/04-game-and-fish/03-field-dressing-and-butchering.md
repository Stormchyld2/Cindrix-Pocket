Meat spoils fast. A clean, quick field dressing and fast cooling decide whether the meat is good.

## Before you start
Wear gloves. Check the animal for signs of disease: staggering, wasting, odd behaviour, or spots on the liver. **Do not eat an animal that looks sick.** Chronic wasting disease (CWD) has been found in deer in both West Virginia and Tennessee. Follow state testing and carcass-movement rules, and do not eat meat from an animal that tests positive. Avoid cutting the brain, spine or lymph glands, and wear gloves.

## Deer: field dressing
1. Tag the animal as the law requires.
2. Roll it on its back, head uphill.
3. Cut a shallow line from breastbone to pelvis, lifting the skin away from the guts so you do not puncture the stomach or intestines.
4. Cut around the vent and free the lower intestine.
5. Reach in, cut the diaphragm, and pull out the windpipe, lungs, heart and organs in one motion.
6. Save the heart and liver if they look healthy.
7. Drain the cavity and wipe it with dry cloths or snow.

## Cool the meat
**Get the deer cooled below 40°F within a few hours.** In warm weather, skin it quickly and put it on ice, or butcher it fast. Hang it in a cool, shaded, fly-proof place. In the cold of November this takes a day or two.

## Butchering
Cut away the hindquarters, front shoulders, backstraps (loins), and tenderloins. Remove silver skin, fat and gristle, because they taste gamey. Grind or cube trimmings for sausage and stew.

## Small game
- **Squirrel and rabbit:** wear gloves. Rabbits can carry tularemia, so avoid eating one that moved slowly or looks sick, and cook until well done (to **165°F**).
- Skin by cutting across the back and pulling both ways, then remove the guts.
- Wash the meat in cold clean water.

## Safe cooking
Cook ground game to **160°F**, whole cuts to **145°F** with a rest, and poultry or turkey to **165°F**. Use a thermometer. Wash hands, knives and boards after handling raw meat.
