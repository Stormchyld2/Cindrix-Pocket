Hunting is a promise: a clean, quick kill, or no shot at all. That means shooting skill first.

## The four safety rules
1. **Treat every firearm as loaded.**
2. **Never point it at anything you are not willing to destroy.**
3. **Keep your finger off the trigger until your sights are on the target and you have decided to fire.**
4. **Know your target, its background and what is beyond it.**
Also: unload when crossing fences, climbing, or entering a vehicle.

## Make the rifle fit
Zero your rifle at 100 yards at a range, then shoot from sitting, kneeling and standing, using a rest or sling. Know the distance at which you can place **every shot in a pie-plate-sized circle** (about 8 inches). That is your maximum range. Most hunters should stay inside **100 yards**.

## The fundamentals
- **Stance and rest:** steady, supported by a tree, log or pack.
- **Sight picture:** clear front sight (or crosshair) on the target.
- **Breathing:** take a breath, let half out, and hold.
- **Trigger squeeze:** slow and straight back; the shot should surprise you.
- **Follow through:** stay on target after the shot.

## Shot placement
Aim for the **heart and lungs** behind the front leg, in the middle of the chest, on a broadside or slightly quartering-away deer. Do not take head or neck shots, running shots or shots through brush. For small game with a .22 or shotgun, aim for the head or the front of the body, depending on the gun.

## Practise
Shoot at a range at least once a month in season. Dry-fire at home with an unloaded, safe gun. Keep ammunition dry and cool.

- Whatever the scope says
