Streams and ponds in Appalachia hold trout, bass, bluegill, crappie, catfish and more. A pond with bluegill and bass can feed a family year after year if you take only a part.

## Fishing
- **Licence:** both states require a fishing licence for most anglers. Check the daily limits and the rules for trout streams.
- **Simple tackle:** a rod and reel or a hand line, hooks, small bobbers, and live worms, crickets or grasshoppers.
- **Bluegill and sunfish** are easy and plentiful. **Catfish** bite at night on stink bait or chicken liver.
- **Cleaning:** scale or skin, cut from vent to gills, remove guts and gills, rinse. Keep fish on ice or in cold water.
- **Cook** to **145°F** or until the flesh is opaque and flakes.

## Take care
Check the state advisories on fish from some rivers, because mercury and other contaminants gather in large, old predators. Eat smaller fish, and eat less of the big ones.

## Keeping meat without power
- **Cold:** in winter hang meat outdoors in a covered, animal-proof place below 40°F.
- **Drying (jerky):** slice lean meat thin, salt it, and dry it with low heat to a leathery, bendable texture. Use only lean meat.
- **Salting and smoking:** the Academy guide on preserving food shows how.
- **Canning:** meat is a low-acid food and needs a pressure canner.
- **Cooked and eaten fast:** when you cannot keep it, share it with neighbours.
