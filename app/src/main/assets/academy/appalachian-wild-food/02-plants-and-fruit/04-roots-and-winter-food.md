In winter the land is bare, but food remains in roots, tubers, nuts you stored and bark.

## Roots and tubers
- **Jerusalem artichoke (sunchoke):** a tall sunflower relative with knobby tubers. Dig after frost. Cook well and eat small amounts at first, since they cause gas.
- **Cattail rootstalk:** starchy roots from clean water, ground into flour. Harvest in late fall through winter.
- **Groundnut (Apios americana):** small tubers on a vine. Cook thoroughly.
- **Wild onions and garlic:** the bulbs. Smell confirms them.

## Hard-looking foods
- **Inner bark of pine, slippery elm and birch:** a famine food, not a meal. Scrape off the cambium (the pale layer under the outer bark), dry and grind into flour. Never strip a tree all the way around: it kills it.
- **Pine needles and spruce tips:** steep for tea. **Never use yew**, which is deadly. Pines have needles in bundles; yew has flat needles and red berries. If in doubt, skip it.

## Plan
Winter is for eating what you stored, hunting and fishing. Do not plan to forage your way through a winter. A family cannot live on bark.
