Fall is the richest season in the Appalachians. Nuts are the most valuable wild food there is: calories, fat and protein that keep.

## The nuts
- **Hickory (shagbark, pignut):** thick husks that split. Crack the shell and eat the kernel. Shagbark is the best.
- **Black walnut:** green husk that stains hands black. Let the husks rot or dry, then crack the shell. Strong flavour.
- **Butternut and hazelnut:** also edible. Squirrels get them fast.
- **American chestnut:** rare after blight; sometimes found as sprouts.
- **Acorns (white oak group, round-lobed leaves):** sweeter. **Red oak group (pointed lobes):** bitter, with more tannin. All acorns need leaching.

## Leaching acorns
1. Shell the acorns and chop or grind them.
2. Soak in cold water, changing it twice a day, until the water runs clear and the nut tastes mild (1 to 3 days for white oak, longer for red).
3. Or boil in several changes of water.
4. Dry the meal. Eat it as mush or flatbread, or add it to soup.
Never eat raw acorns in large amounts: tannins cause stomach pain.

## Fruits
- **Persimmon:** eat only when fully ripe and soft; unripe persimmons are puckering and can cause stomach blockages.
- **Wild apples, crabapples and paw paws:** pick, cook and enjoy.
- **Wild grapes:** sour but good cooked.

## Storing
Dry nuts in the shell in a cool, dry, rodent-proof place. They keep through winter.
