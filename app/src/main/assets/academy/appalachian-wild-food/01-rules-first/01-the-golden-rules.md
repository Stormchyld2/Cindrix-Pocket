Wild food is a skill that kills the careless. Before any plant, nut, mushroom or animal, learn these rules.

## The rules
1. **Be certain.** If you are not 100 percent sure of a plant, do not eat it. Not 95 percent. Certain.
2. **Know the look-alikes.** Every good wild food has a bad twin. Learn the twin first.
3. **Use at least three features** (leaf, stem, flower or fruit, smell, habitat, season) to confirm each plant. One feature is not enough.
4. **Learn in person.** A book and a local teacher beat any app. Go with someone experienced in each season.
5. **Start small.** Eat a thumb-sized portion of anything new, wait a day, and write it down. Some people react to foods that are safe for others.
6. **Never eat from** roadsides, old orchards, ditches, lawns that were sprayed, mine drainage, or near old buildings and dumps. Plants hold what is in the soil.
7. **Take little.** Leave most of every patch so it grows back. Never strip a plant bare.
8. **Wash and cook** most things. Cooking kills germs and breaks down many natural poisons.
9. **When in doubt, throw it out.**

## Do not rely on "tests"
The "universal edibility test" (touch, taste, wait) is not safe: some deadly plants and mushrooms taste mild and act slowly. Rely on certain identification, not on tasting.

## Where this course applies
It is written for the hills of West Virginia and east, middle and west Tennessee. Plants and seasons shift with elevation and from year to year, so use the calendar as a guide, not a promise.

## If someone eats the wrong thing
Poison control: **1-800-222-1222** (US). Keep a sample of what was eaten. Mushroom illness that starts 6 hours or more after eating is the most dangerous kind. See the card "Poisoning or swallowed something".
