Wild food is regulated for good reasons: to keep game populations healthy and to keep you safe. In a true collapse the rules may not be enforced, but until then they apply, and following them keeps you out of trouble and keeps the land stocked.

## Licences and seasons
- **West Virginia:** hunting and fishing need a licence from the Division of Natural Resources (WVDNR). Deer, bear, turkey, squirrel and other game each have set seasons, bag limits, and tagging or check-in rules.
- **Tennessee:** the Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency (TWRA) sets seasons, bag limits and licences, and many species must be reported.
- Both states require a **hunter education** course for most hunters. Take it. It is the best safety course there is.
- **Check the current rules every year.** Seasons, limits and chronic wasting disease zones change.

## Land
- **Public land:** the Monongahela, George Washington, Cherokee and Nantahala National Forests, state forests and wildlife management areas have their own rules. Some allow picking small amounts for personal use; many forbid digging roots or ginseng.
- **Private land:** always ask the owner. Take only what they say.
- **Parks:** most national and state parks forbid taking plants or game at all.

## Ginseng and other protected plants
Wild American ginseng, ramps in some parks and several other plants are protected. Do not dig or sell them without checking the rules.

## In a true emergency
If you must feed a family and the system is gone, take what you need, take it from the surplus (the old, males and the abundant), and leave breeding stock. Fish and game laws exist to prevent exactly the crash you would suffer if everyone over-hunted at once.
