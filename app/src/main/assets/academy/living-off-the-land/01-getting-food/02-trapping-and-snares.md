Traps work while you do something else. In normal times trapping is licensed and regulated: check state rules. In a true emergency the same methods feed a family.

## Principles
- **Place traps on trails:** runways, den entrances, and water edges where signs of animals are fresh (tracks, droppings, chewed plants).
- **Make it humane:** a trap should kill fast. Use traps that match the animal.
- **Hide your scent:** wear gloves and keep traps clean.
- **Check daily:** a trap left for days suffers an animal and spoils the meat.
- **Use bait** that fits the animal: nuts, grain, vegetables, fish scraps.

## Simple options
- **Cage (box) trap:** a live trap for rabbits and squirrels.
- **Snare:** a wire or cord loop on a trail. Wire is better than cord.
- **Deadfall (figure-4):** a heavy weight held up by sticks that falls when the bait is moved. Dangerous to set: practise with a lightweight stone.
- **Fish trap:** a stone V in a stream, or a funnel trap of sticks or mesh.

## Safety and law
Keep traps away from paths people and pets use. Do not set traps where children or dogs play. Licences, seasons and methods are set by the state, and some methods are illegal.

## After the catch
Handle the animal with gloves and cook the meat well. See "Field dressing" in the wild food course.

? Where should you place a snare?

+ On a fresh animal trail
- Next to your house

> Animals use regular runs.

? How often should you check traps?
- Once a week
- Once a month
- Never
+ Every day
> Left traps cause suffering and spoil meat.

? Why wear gloves when setting traps?
- It looks better
+ It hides your scent and protects you from disease
- It keeps traps warm
- It is required for bait
> Animals avoid human scent and can carry disease.
