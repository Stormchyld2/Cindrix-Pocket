A household uses 3 to 8 cords of wood a winter in the Appalachians if wood is the only heat. A cord is a stack 4 feet high, 4 feet deep and 8 feet long.

## Plan
- **Cut standing dead and fallen wood first.** Take live trees only with permission.
- **Season it.** Split wood dries in 6 to 12 months in a stack off the ground, open to wind, with a cover on top.
- **Hardwoods** (oak, hickory, maple, locust) burn long. **Softwoods** (pine) burn fast and sooty, so use them for kindling.
- **Stack** the wood so air flows through, and keep it away from the house wall.

## Safety
- Clean chimneys and stovepipes every year.
- Use a carbon monoxide alarm.
- Cut with a sharp saw, eye and ear protection and chaps. A chainsaw takes fuel and skill; a bow saw and axe need neither.

## Other fuel
Charcoal, dried dung, and small branches. Never burn treated lumber, painted wood or trash indoors.
