Metal repair keeps tools, fences, wheels, stoves and roofs working.

## Safe work
- **Eye protection:** goggles for grinding and a proper welding helmet for welding. Never look at an arc with bare eyes.
- **Skin:** leather gloves, long cotton or wool clothes (not synthetic), and boots.
- **Fire:** clear flammable material, keep a bucket of water and sand.
- **Fumes:** work outdoors or with good airflow. **Never heat or weld galvanised (zinc-coated) metal, because the fumes cause metal fume fever.** Do not weld painted, plated or unknown metal.
- Wear a respirator if you can.

## Cold work
Use a hacksaw, files, a vise, a drill and a hammer to cut, shape, join with bolts and rivets, and straighten. Cold metal is the safest to work.

## A simple forge
A hot fire in a pot of charcoal, fed with air from a bellows or a hair-dryer-sized blower, heats steel to orange. Hammer on an anvil or a block of steel, reheating as it cools. Quench carbon steel in water or oil. Practise on mild steel first.

## Welding
Arc welding needs electric power: a generator or the grid. Practise on scrap to learn the arc and the bead. Without power, learn to rivet, bolt, braze and forge-weld.

## What to keep
Rods, bolts, nuts, wire, nails and screws in sorted jars. Scrap steel for mending.

## Do it
- [ ] Cut, drill and bolt together a steel bracket using cold work only.
- [ ] Forge a nail or a hook from mild steel, with eye protection and leather gloves.
