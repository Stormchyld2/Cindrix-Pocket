Hand tools need no power, and they keep working when the grid fails.

## Core tools
Handsaw (cross-cut and rip), hatchet or axe, chisel, hand plane, brace and bit or hand drill, mallet, square, tape measure, pencil, files and clamps.

## Basics
- **Measure twice, cut once.** Mark with a pencil and a square.
- **Saw** on the waste side of the line, with long, light strokes. Let the saw do the work.
- **Chisel** away from your body, with your other hand behind the blade, never in front.
- **Plane** with the grain, in smooth strokes.
- **Joints:** simple lap, butt (with screws or pegs), and mortise and tenon for strength.
- **Pegs and glue:** hardwood pegs and hide glue hold furniture together for centuries.

## Green wood and seasoned wood
Green wood is easier to cut and shape but shrinks as it dries. Use seasoned wood for furniture and tool handles. Hickory and ash make strong handles.

## Safety
Clamp work down, keep tools sharp, wear safety glasses, and work in good light. A dull tool is a dangerous tool.

## Projects that matter
Tool handles, shelves, a table and chairs, a door and a gate, a bed frame, a chicken coop and a rain barrel stand.
