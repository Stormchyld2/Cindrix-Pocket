A sharp tool is safer and does more with less effort. Sharpening is the first repair skill to learn.

## Knives
1. Use a whetstone, wet with water or a little oil.
2. Hold the blade at about **20 degrees** to the stone (the thickness of two coins under the spine).
3. Push the edge across the stone as if slicing a thin layer off, keeping the same angle.
4. Do the same number of strokes on each side.
5. Finish with a few light strokes on a finer stone or a leather strop.
6. Test on paper or the back of a thumbnail (never on skin).

## Axes
Use a flat file or stone, working from the blade into the edge, away from your body. Keep a "toe to heel" taper, and never grind the edge to a thin blade.

## Saws
Use a triangular file to touch up each tooth at its original angle, working every other tooth from one side and then the other.

## Safety
Move the stone, not the knife, if you can. Keep fingers behind the edge. Wear gloves for large blades.

## Check yourself

? What angle for a knife on a stone?
- 90 degrees
- 45 degrees
+ About 20 degrees
- 5 degrees
> Around 20 degrees makes a strong sharp edge.

? Why is a sharp tool safer?
- It is lighter
+ You push less, so it slips less
- It is shinier
- It cuts slower
> Force causes slips.

? What does a strop do?
- Cuts steel
- Cleans rust
+ Polishes and aligns the edge
- Heats it
> A strop refines the edge.
