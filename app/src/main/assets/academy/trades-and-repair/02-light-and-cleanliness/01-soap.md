Soap kills fewer germs than people think, but it **removes** them. Washing hands and dishes prevents more sickness than any medicine.

## Lye safety first
Soap is made with **lye** (sodium hydroxide), which burns skin and eyes and blinds.
- Wear **goggles and rubber gloves** and long sleeves.
- Work outdoors or with ventilation and keep children and pets away.
- **Always add lye to water, never water to lye**, slowly, stirring, in a plastic or stainless container. It gets very hot and gives off fumes.
- Keep vinegar nearby for splashes on surfaces (rinse skin with water for 15 minutes and get help for eyes).

## Basic method
1. Measure fat (lard, tallow, vegetable oil) and lye by weight, using a lye calculator for the fat you have, because each fat needs a different amount.
2. Dissolve the lye in water and let it cool to about 100°F.
3. Warm the fat to about the same temperature.
4. Pour the lye water slowly into the fat, stirring until it thickens ("trace").
5. Pour into a mould, cover, and keep warm for a day.
6. Cut into bars and let them **cure for 4 to 6 weeks** until mild.

## Lye from ashes
Pour water through hardwood ashes in a barrel and collect the liquid. This gives weak lye of uncertain strength. Test it (it should float an egg partway) and use it in country soap. Expect trial and error.

## Without lye
Plants like soapwort and yucca give a mild, soapy lather.
