## Read this first: what a long emergency is
A long emergency is when the power, water, shops, fuel and help you rely on stay away for weeks, months or longer: a grid failure, a severe economic or supply collapse, a pandemic, a long disaster recovery. Nobody can say how long it will last, so the plan is built to be steady, not fast.
- Plan for the long case, and be glad if it ends early.
- Water first, then warmth and shelter, then safety, then food. People die of thirst, cold and infection long before hunger.
- Do the next right thing. Do not try to fix everything on day one.

## Decide: stay or go
go: Plans
button: Open the evacuation plan
Most people are safer staying where they have shelter, water, tools and neighbours. Go only if staying is more dangerous: fire, flood, fallout, no way to get water or heat, or a credible threat you cannot stand against. Decide early, while roads and fuel still allow it, and agree the destination, route and a second route before you need them.
- Write down what would make you leave: your triggers.
- If you stay, you are committing to the rest of this plan.

## Day 1: stop, count, steady
go: Dashboard
button: Open the Dashboard
Check everyone is safe and warm. Find out what is really happening from a radio, not from rumour. Shut off what is damaged (gas, water, power) and put out any fire risks. Then count: people, water, food, fuel, medicine, light, batteries. Do not waste anything until you know the count.
- Fill every clean container with water while the taps still run.
- Keep the freezer and fridge shut; eat what spoils first.
- Charge every battery you can while there is still power.

## Day 1 to 3: water comes first
go: Cards
button: Open the Cards
Plan on a gallon (about four litres) per person per day for drinking and basic cooking, more in heat and for the sick, and more again for washing. Find your sources before the stored water runs low: rain catchment, a spring, a creek, a pond, a well. Every source water gets treated: filter, then boil or disinfect.
check: water
- Never drink untreated water, even if it looks clear.
- Keep drinking water in clean covered containers, away from sunlight.
- Wash hands with treated water and soap before eating and after the toilet.

## Day 1 to 3: warmth and shelter
Choose one warm room, shrink to it in cold weather, and keep it dry. Heat only with equipment made for indoors, with fresh air and a working carbon monoxide alarm. Never run a generator, grill or camp stove inside or in a garage.
- Dress in layers, keep your head, hands and feet covered, and keep clothing dry.
- Sleep off the floor, and in a group on cold nights.
- Watch the elderly, babies and anyone ill: they chill fastest.

## Week 1: a routine and a watch
Routine keeps people steady and keeps the work done. Set a simple day: wake, water, meals, chores, rest, a watch, a talk at the end of the day. Write who does what and when. Rotate the hard and boring jobs.
- Meet briefly each day: what happened, what is low, what is next.
- Keep a log: date, weather, water used, food used, problems, decisions.
- Rest is part of the plan. Tired people make dangerous mistakes.

## Week 1: sanitation before sickness
go: Cards
button: Open the Cards
Sanitation prevents more illness than medicine can cure. Keep waste away from water and food: a bucket toilet with a lid and a carbon cover, or a trench latrine well downhill and far from any water source. Wash hands, boil or bleach dishes, burn or bury rubbish, control flies and rats.
check: lesson:water-and-sanitation/03-store-and-sanitation/02-emergency-toilets-and-waste
- Anyone with vomiting or diarrhoea is kept apart, given fluids, and their waste handled carefully.
- A sick person drinks little and often; babies and the elderly dehydrate quickest.

## Week 1: what runs out first
go: What fails
button: Open What fails
Count water, food, fuel, medicine and light, and see which runs out first. That one sets the clock. Ration the thing that runs out first, and start replacing it now.
- Ration by plan, not by mood: same share for all, with extra for children, pregnancy, hard work and illness.
- Do not hide the count. People cope better with a hard truth than with a surprise.

## Week 1: power, light and batteries
go: Academy
button: Open the Academy
Use less before you make more. Lamps and candles for light, a small solar panel or hand crank for radios and phones, a battery bank kept above half charge. Treat every battery and charger as precious.
check: lesson:power-outage-preparedness/03-settle-in-for-more-than-one-day/03-backup-power-done-safely
- Generators: outdoors only, far from windows, fuel stored safely, run them for set hours only.
- Know what you really need power for: medicine cooling, radio, water pump, a little light.

## Week 1: communications and information
Set up your communications in order: primary, alternate, contingency, emergency (the PACE plan; answers go in Settings, PACE plan). A battery or crank radio for news. Agree check-in times and channels. Keep radio talk short, and use plain words.
check: lesson:communications/02-comms-pace-plan/02-how-to-build-a-pace-plan
- Listen first, transmit rarely: transmitting tells others where you are.
- Write down what you hear and when. Treat rumour as unconfirmed.

## Week 1: people who need extra care
Make a list of everyone who needs more: babies and young children, pregnant women, the elderly, people with long-term illness or disability, people who need medicine or equipment that needs power. Plan for each by name.
go: Family
button: Open the Family page
- Children need routine, honesty at their level, jobs, and play.
- Keep a written list of each person's medicines and conditions. Do not change anyone's medicine without a clinician. If a supply is running out, plan with a clinician or pharmacist while you still can.

## Week 2: security that is calm and boring
go: Academy
button: Open the Academy
Good security is mostly habits: lock up, keep light and noise low, know who is on your site, go out in pairs, tell someone where you are going and when you will be back. Avoid confrontation: be the hard target and the quiet one.
check: lesson:home-and-site-security/03-people-and-rules/02-household-security-plan-and-drills
- A watch keeps everyone safer than any lock: two people, short shifts, a handover.
- Agree in advance what you will do if someone comes to the gate, and who decides.
- Do not advertise what you have.

## Week 2: look after the mind
go: Music
button: Open Music
Fear, boredom, grief and anger are normal in a long emergency. Keep a rhythm, give everyone a real job, and have something to look forward to each week: a meal, a game, a song, a talk. Watch for the person who goes quiet. Talk to them.
- A calm voice is the most useful tool you have.
- If someone talks about wanting to die, stay with them and get help; do not leave them alone.

## Month 1: count again and replace what runs out
go: What fails
button: See what replaces it
Stored food ends. Begin now the things that take a season: a garden, preserved food, wild food, trade, small livestock if you can feed them. Check each line of "What replaces it" and mark where you stand. Start the slow ones first.
check: food
- Plan calories, not just meals: an adult needs roughly 2,000 a day or more for heavy work.
- Use the spoil-first food and the fresh food while you have it; keep the long-life food for later.

## Month 1: grow, gather, preserve
go: Seed vault
button: Open the Seed vault
Seeds, beds and know-how take a season. Start beds as soon as the weather allows, plant what grows fast and keeps well, and save your own seed from open-pollinated plants. Preserve a surplus by drying, canning (only with tested methods), fermenting, salting and cool storage.
check: lesson:food-production-and-preservation/02-preserve/01-canning-safely
- Canning low-acid food wrongly can cause botulism: use tested, pressure-canning methods or choose drying and fermenting.
- Keep seed dry, cool, labelled and in two places.

## Month 1: fuel and wood
check: lesson:living-off-the-land/03-the-long-view/01-firewood-and-fuel
Heating and cooking take more fuel than people expect. Cut and stack firewood a year ahead so it is dry. Use efficient stoves, cook once for many, and bank the fire at night. Keep ash away from anything that burns.
- Never burn treated or painted wood, plastics or trash inside.
- Know a chimney is clean before a winter of heavy use.

## Month 1 to 3: wild food and the land around you
go: Academy
button: Open the Academy
Learn a few safe wild foods near you, one at a time, with someone who knows them. Never eat a plant or mushroom you cannot name with certainty. Fishing, snares and hunting help, within the law that applies where you are.
check: lesson:appalachian-wild-food/01-rules-first/01-the-golden-rules
- Learn the poisonous look-alikes first.
- Do not rely on wild food for calories early on: it is a supplement and a skill.

## Month 2: health, hygiene and teeth
go: Cards
button: Open the Cards
Keep up the small things: handwashing, clean water, clean wounds, clean clothes, a bath when you can, brushing teeth, trimming nails, airing bedding. Treat small injuries at once: infections are the common killer in a long emergency. Know the signs that mean "get help now".
check: lesson:the-long-stay/03-resilience/01-health-sanitation-and-teeth
- Clean any wound with clean water, cover it, and check it daily for spreading redness, heat, swelling, pus, fever or red streaks.
- Keep your first aid supplies counted, and know who can do what.

## Month 2: mend and make
go: Academy
button: Open the Academy
When you cannot buy it, you keep it going: sharpen, sew, patch, oil, glue, weld, whittle. Keep spare parts, thread, needles, fasteners, cordage and a few good tools, and teach each skill to a second person.
check: lesson:trades-and-repair/01-edges-and-cloth/01-sharpening

## Month 2: work with neighbours
Share skills, watch together, trade fairly, and check on the vulnerable. Most people help when asked; a few need watching. Agree small rules early, so trust has something to stand on.
go: Plans
button: Open Plans
check: lesson:the-long-stay/02-people-and-trade/02-trade-and-community
- Trade useful things for useful things and keep the terms plain.
- Do not trade away what you will need next winter.

## Month 2: money, papers and records
Keep your important papers, cash in small bills, and a record of who owes what. Banks and cards may not work; barter and favours do. Keep a written record of supplies and decisions: it settles arguments and shows what is working.
- Keep copies of identity, medical and property papers together, dry and safe.

## Every week: check the count
every: 7
Count water, food, fuel, medicine and batteries. Compare with the plan. Fix the one thing that is going wrong now, then go back to the routine.

## Every week: the group meeting
every: 7
Fifteen minutes, everyone: what worked, what did not, what we need, who needs help, what we do next week. Write down the decisions.

## Every month: drill one thing
every: 30
go: Drills
button: Open Drills
Practise one drill that matches your weakest point: fire, a medical emergency, a gate visitor, a radio failure, leaving fast. A skill not practised fades.

## When something goes wrong: recover and learn
A failed harvest, a broken tool, a person's illness, a break-in: say so, plan the fix, and write down what you learned. Recovery is part of the plan, not a sign it failed. After any serious event, hold a short review: what happened, why, what to change.
check: lesson:admin-leadership/03-after-action-review/01-the-after-action-review

## Reduce the next loss
Each failure shows a weak spot. Spend the next free day on it: a second water source, a spare tool, another person trained, a better lock, a drier store. Prevention beats rescue.

## Months 3 to 6: build the lasting system
go: Plans
button: Open Plans
When the first weeks are steady, follow "The first year" for the season-by-season order, and then "Year two and after". By now you should have: a reliable water source and treatment, a safe toilet and waste system, a heat plan, a garden under way, a food store being used wisely, a watch, a routine, and a way to talk to neighbours.

## Winter: hold steady
Winter is when stored food, fuel and morale are tested. Keep warm, keep dry, keep moving, keep company. Do indoor work: mending, learning, planning the garden. Check carbon monoxide alarms and chimneys again. Watch for cabin fever and give people space.

## Spring: restart the cycle
Check seed, tools and beds. Start slow crops early. Check the roof, drains and water catchment. Review what the winter cost, and set the year's goals.

## Summer: grow, store, stay cool
Water needs go up in the heat. Work early and late, rest at midday, watch for heat illness. Preserve everything you can while it is fresh.

## Autumn: harvest and prepare
Harvest, dry, can and store. Gather nuts. Cut and stack wood. Mend the shelter. Plan for the winter count.

## When someone dies
Death in a long emergency is hard and must be handled with care, for the person, the family and your water. Follow the plan "Remains management and water protection (SOP)" and the card "Dealing with a death when no help comes".
go: Plans
button: Open Plans
- Record who, when and where, take a photograph of the face and note any ID, wrap the body and keep it cold.
- Allow a farewell and the customs the person would have wanted.
- Do not bury from a distance rule alone. Check the soil, groundwater, drainage, karst and the law first. Limestone country can carry contamination far underground.
- Keep a written record and a map.

## Know when to move
go: Plans
button: Open the evacuation plan
Move only for a good reason: a source of water or food that cannot be had here, a danger you cannot meet, a place that is clearly better and reachable. Go in daylight, in company, with a plan, a route, and a place to arrive. Leave a note where friends can find it.

Teach every skill to a second and a third person. Write down what you learn: seed dates, water levels, what grew, what failed, who can do what. A group that records and teaches survives losing a member.
