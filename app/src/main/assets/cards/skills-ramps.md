**Only pick a ramp if it smells strongly of onion or garlic.**
1. Look in rich, damp woods in spring for two or three broad, smooth, flat leaves from one stem, with a purple-red stem base.
2. **Crush a leaf and smell it.** It must smell of onion or garlic.
3. Take **one leaf per plant** and leave the bulb, so the patch survives.
4. Check the park and forest rules first: many places forbid taking ramps.
5. Wash and cook or eat in small amounts.

## If you have less gear

- **No smell:** do not eat it.
- **No knife:** pinch a leaf off at the base.

## Know more

### Look-alikes
**Lily of the valley** and **false hellebore (corn lily)** have similar leaves but **no onion smell** and are poisonous. Never trust looks alone.

### Protect the patch
Ramps take years to regrow. Over-harvesting has wiped out patches.

### When
April to May.

**Train:** The Academy course "Wild food of the Appalachians".

Source: WVU Extension and University of Tennessee Extension foraging guides; Peterson Field Guide to Edible Wild Plants
