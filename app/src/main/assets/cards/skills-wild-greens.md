**Spring greens are safe and common, but only when you are certain of them.**
1. Find **dandelion**: a single hollow stalk per flower, and a basal rosette of toothed leaves. Eat young leaves, flowers and roots.
2. Find **chickweed**: low, sprawling, with tiny star flowers and one line of hairs on the stem.
3. Pick from clean ground, away from roads, sprays and dumps.
4. Wash well, and boil older greens for 5 to 10 minutes in a change of water.
5. Eat a small amount the first time and wait a day.

## If you have less gear

- **No pot:** young leaves can be eaten raw in small amounts.
- **No guide:** go out with someone who knows them. Learn in person.

## Know more

### Look-alikes
Hawkweed and cat's ear look like dandelion and are edible. Any plant with **milky sap, umbrella-shaped flower clusters, or a bitter or soapy taste** should be skipped. Water hemlock is one of the deadliest plants in North America.

### When
March to May in West Virginia and Tennessee, a little later at elevation.

### Have ready
A basket, a knife, clean water and a pot.

**Train:** The Academy course "Wild food of the Appalachians".

Source: WVU Extension and University of Tennessee Extension foraging guides; Peterson Field Guide to Edible Wild Plants
