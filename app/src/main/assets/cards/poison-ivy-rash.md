1. Wash skin with soap and cool water as soon as you can. Wash clothes, tools and pet fur that touched the plant.
2. Do not scratch. Cool compresses and cool baths ease the itch.
3. **Never burn these plants:** the smoke can inflame the lungs.

**Call 911** for trouble breathing or swelling of the face and throat. See a doctor for a rash on the face, eyes, mouth or private areas, or a rash over a large part of the body.

## If no help is coming

- **Cool water, then oatmeal baths,** and calamine lotion or a hydrocortisone cream as the label says.
- **Rash lasts 1 to 3 weeks.** It spreads over days because the oil reaches skin at different times, not from the blisters.
- **Wash everything the oil touched:** clothing, boots, tool handles, pets. The oil stays active for a long time.
- **Plant ID:** poison ivy has three leaflets, poison oak has lobed leaves, sumac has many leaflets on one stem.
- **Infected:** pus, spreading redness or fever. See "Signs a wound is infected".

## Know more

### Why it itches
An oil called urushiol triggers an allergy in most people.

### Avoiding it
Long sleeves and gloves in brush. Wash skin within 30 minutes if exposed.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".

Source: American Academy of Dermatology and CDC (NIOSH) guidance.
