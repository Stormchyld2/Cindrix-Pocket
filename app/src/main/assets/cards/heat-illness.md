**Heat stroke is an emergency:** body very hot, confusion, slurred speech, stumbling, passing out, a seizure. The skin may be dry or still sweating.

1. **Cool them now.** Move to shade. Take off outer clothing.
2. **The fastest way is water:** lay them in a stream, pond or tub of cool water up to the neck, holding the head up. If there is none, soak them and their clothes with water and fan hard. Put cold wet cloths on the neck, armpits and groin.
3. If phones work, call 911. **Keep cooling while you wait.**
4. Give sips of water only when they are fully awake.

**Heat exhaustion** (heavy sweating, pale, weak, dizzy, headache, sick, cramps, but thinking clearly): rest lying down in the shade, loosen clothing, wet the skin, and sip water or a salted drink. They should improve within an hour.

**Do not** give fever medicine for heat stroke. It does not work and can harm.

## If no help is coming

- **Cooling is the whole treatment** and you can do it as well as a hospital can. Keep going until they are thinking clearly again, then stop so they do not get chilled.
- **Check every 10 minutes** while cooling: are they making sense, breathing, pulse.
- **After heat stroke** the body cannot control its temperature for days. Rest in the shade for at least a week, no work in the heat, and plenty to drink.
- **Watch the urine** for 3 days. Dark brown urine means muscle damage clogging the kidneys: push fluids until it runs pale.
- **Getting worse:** confusion returning, no urine, yellowing eyes, bleeding gums, breathing trouble. These need a hospital if one can be reached.

## Know more

### Telling the two apart
The test is the brain. A person who is hot and miserable but thinking clearly has heat exhaustion. A person who is confused, clumsy, angry for no reason or not making sense has heat stroke, whatever the skin feels like. When unsure, cool them as for heat stroke.

### Heat cramps
Painful spasms in the legs or belly after sweating hard. Rest in the shade, stretch the muscle gently, and drink water with salt in it (a quarter teaspoon to a quart).

### Too much water
Drinking large amounts of plain water for hours while sweating can thin the salt in the blood: headache, swollen fingers, sickness, then confusion. Add salty food or a salted drink to the water.

### Who is at most risk
Babies, old people, the overweight, anyone on heart or mood medicine, anyone not used to the heat, and anyone working hard in protective clothing. It takes about 10 days of gradually harder work to get used to heat.

### Working in heat
Work in the cool of morning and evening. Rest in shade for 10 minutes each hour. Drink before you are thirsty. Watch each other: the person with heat stroke does not notice it. See the card "Staying safe in heat outdoors".
