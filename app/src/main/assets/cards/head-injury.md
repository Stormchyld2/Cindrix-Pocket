**Danger signs after a blow to the head** (call 911 at once if phones work): knocked out, cannot be woken, one pupil bigger than the other, a headache that gets worse, repeated vomiting, a seizure, slurred speech, weakness or numbness, growing confusion or restlessness, clear fluid or blood from the nose or ears.

1. Keep the person still and calm. If the neck may be hurt, **do not move them** (see the card "Neck or back injury").
2. Stop any bleeding with gentle pressure. Press around, not on, a dent or soft spot in the skull.
3. Put a cold pack wrapped in cloth on swelling.
4. Stay with them and watch them closely for the next day.

**Do not** give alcohol or sleeping pills, and do not let them go back to hard work, sport or driving the same day.

## If no help is coming

- **Watch for 48 hours.** Most bleeding inside the skull shows itself in the first day, some on the second.
- **Check every hour for the first 6 hours, then every 2 to 3 hours,** including through the first night. Wake them each time. They should rouse normally, know who and where they are, and move both arms and legs.
- **Rest with the head and shoulders raised** a little. Quiet, dim room. Sips and light food if they are not vomiting.
- **Pain:** a plain pain reliever by the label is acceptable. Avoid ones that thin the blood if you have a choice.
- **What you cannot fix:** bleeding inside the skull needs a surgeon. If the danger signs appear and there is any hospital within reach, go, keeping the head and neck in line.
- **Getting better:** headache easing, thinking clearer, no vomiting after the first hours.

## Know more

### Concussion
A knock that causes a short blackout, a dazed spell, or no memory of the event. Expect headache, dizziness, feeling sick, tiredness and trouble concentrating for days.
- Days 1 and 2: rest, little reading, no effort.
- Then add light activity a step at a time. If the headache comes back, drop back a step.
- **No second blow.** Keep them away from any risk of another head knock until every symptom has been gone for a week. A second blow before the brain recovers can kill.

### Cuts on the scalp
The scalp bleeds a lot from small cuts. Press firmly for 10 minutes. Once it stops, clean it and close a gaping cut by tying strands of hair across it or with tape strips.

### Signs of a skull fracture
Clear or bloody fluid from the nose or an ear, bruising behind the ear or around both eyes appearing over a day, or a dent you can feel. Do not plug the ear or nose. Cover loosely and let it drain.

### Children and babies
A child who cries at once, then settles and plays normally, is usually fine. Worry about a child who is unusually sleepy, will not stop crying, will not feed, vomits more than twice, or has a soft swelling on the skull. A baby's soft spot bulging is a danger sign.

### Old people and blood thinners
Bleeding can show up slowly, over days or even weeks, after a knock that seemed minor. Watch them for longer.
