When you are close to danger, or cannot be heard, signals let a group move together. This is a suggested set: any signals work if **everyone knows them** and practices.
1. **Stop:** clenched fist held beside your head.
2. **Go:** point forward and swing your arm in a forward arc.
3. **Slow down:** palm down, press the air gently.
4. **Quiet:** finger across the lips.
5. **Danger ahead:** hand flat across the throat, then point toward it.
6. **Look or listen:** point to your eye or ear, then toward what you mean.
7. **Gather here:** circle a raised hand above your head.
8. **Spread out or close up:** arms apart or hands together.
9. **Agree the replies,** such as a thumb up for "I understand."

## If you have less gear

- **No light:** touch signals (a squeeze on the arm) work in the dark.
- **Far apart:** whistle signals, or a bright cloth on a pole.
- **Children:** a simple game with three signals is enough.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Too many signals, signals not practiced, and no reply signal.

### Practice
Practice in daylight, then in low light, until nobody asks what a signal means.

### Leader and tail
Keep the leader and the last person in sight of each other.

**Train:** Academy course "Operator movement and evasion".

Source: Adapted from the hand-and-arm signals in the US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76); a civilian set, simplified.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
