A **tornado watch** means conditions could make one. A **warning** means one is happening or coming: take shelter now.
1. Go **right away** to a safe room, basement or storm cellar, or a small interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building. Stay away from windows and outside walls.
2. Cover your head and neck, with a mattress, helmet or heavy coat if you can.
3. **Mobile or manufactured home:** it is not safe. Go to a sturdy building before the storm.
4. **In a car:** park out of traffic, stay buckled in, head below the windows. Never shelter under an overpass.
5. After: **if phones work, text, and call 911** for injuries. Stay clear of downed power lines.

## If no help is coming

- **No siren?** Watch the sky: a dark green sky, large hail, a loud steady roar, or a wall cloud. Go to shelter at once.
- **No basement:** lowest floor, interior room, in a bathtub, under a mattress.
- **After:** check everyone for injury and treat bleeding first. See "Severe bleeding".
- **Gas, wires and glass:** smell for gas, avoid downed lines, wear thick shoes and gloves.
- **Trapped:** whistle or tap. Do not shout.
- **Shelter:** a root cellar or buried shelter is a simple long-term answer. Stock it with water, a light, a radio and a first aid kit.

## Know more

### Watch or warning
A watch: get ready. A warning: go now.

### Where it hits
Strong tornadoes often come in the afternoon and evening. Spring is the peak.

**Train:** Academy course "First 72 hours".
