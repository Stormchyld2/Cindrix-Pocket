**Priority:** keep out of the wind and wet and **off the cold ground.** Heat leaves your body faster into the ground than into the air.
1. **Choose the site:** dry, flat, near water but not in a gully or floodplain, away from dead branches and rock slides, out of the wind.
2. **Insulate underneath** first: thick layers of leaves, pine boughs or grass, at least a hand's width thick.
3. **Lean-to:** a strong pole between two trees or on forked sticks, branches leaned against it at a steep angle, covered with leaves, bark or a tarp.
4. **Debris hut:** a ridge pole propped on a stump, ribs on both sides, and **very thick** leaves or grass over the top (arm's length or more). Just big enough for your body.
5. **Tarp or poncho:** tie a line between two trees, drape the cover, peg the corners and angle away from the wind.
6. **Snow:** dig a trench or cave with a small air hole, and sit on insulation.
7. **Keep it small.** A small shelter is warmer.

## If you have less gear

- **No tarp:** bark slabs, branches and thick debris. A car or building is a ready-made shelter.
- **No cord:** use vines, strips of bark or cloth.
- **No time:** a pile of leaves with you in it beats nothing. Under a fallen tree is a start.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Too big, too thin on top, bare ground underneath, and camping in a hollow where cold air settles.

### Practice
Build one in the yard and sleep in it before you need it.

**Train:** Academy course "Cold-weather living".
