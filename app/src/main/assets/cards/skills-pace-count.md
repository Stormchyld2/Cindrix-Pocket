1. **Measure your pace.** On flat ground, lay out 100 meters (about 328 feet). Walk it naturally and count **every second step** (each time your right foot lands). Do it a few times and average. Many people get around 60 to 70 double steps per 100 meters.
2. **Write it on a card.** It **changes:** more steps uphill, in sand, snow or mud, with a pack, or when tired.
3. **Count in your head in hundreds,** and move a pebble or knot a bead every 100 meters.
4. **Distance:** paces walked divided by your paces per 100 meters, times 100 meters.
5. **Time works too.** Many people walk about 4 kilometers (2.5 miles) per hour on level ground, about half that in rough country.
6. Keep a **running note** of bearing and distance for each leg.

## If you have less gear

- **No tape:** pace out a football field (about 100 yards) or use a known road distance.
- **No pebbles:** knots in a cord, marks in the dirt, or beads on a shoelace.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Losing the count, and not changing the count for hills and load.

### Practice
Pace-count a known distance with and without a pack.

**Train:** Academy course "Land navigation".

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased.
