1. Get out of the sun. Cool the skin with cool (not ice-cold) water or damp cloths.
2. Drink extra water. Sunburn pulls water from the body.
3. Use a plain moisturizer or aloe. No petroleum jelly or numbing sprays on a fresh burn.
4. Leave blisters alone.

**If phones work, call 911** for confusion, fainting, or a baby under one year with a sunburn. See a doctor for blisters over a large area or a burn with fever or chills.

## If no help is coming

- **Large blistered burns** are treated like other burns. See "Burns". Keep the skin clean and covered with a non-stick dressing.
- **Sun and heat together:** look for heat illness: headache, dizziness, nausea, fast pulse. See "Heat exhaustion and heat stroke".
- **Fluids and salt** back in the body. Rest in the shade.
- **Peeling skin:** do not pick. Moisturize.
- **Cover up until healed.** Burned skin burns faster.

## Know more

### Prevention
Long sleeves, a wide hat, shade in the middle of the day. Burns come faster at altitude, near water and snow.

### Without sunscreen
Clothing is the best protection. A thick layer of clay or mud also blocks sun in a pinch.
