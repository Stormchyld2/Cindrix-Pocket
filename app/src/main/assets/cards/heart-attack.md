Signs (men and women): chest pain or pressure in the center or left side that lasts more than a few minutes or comes and goes; weak, light-headed or faint; cold sweat; pain in the jaw, neck, back, one or both arms or shoulders; shortness of breath. Women are more likely to have nausea or vomiting and unusual tiredness.

1. If phones work, **call 911 right away.** Do not wait to see if it passes. Earlier treatment saves heart muscle.
2. **Stop all effort.** Sit the person down, half lying back with knees bent and the back supported. Loosen tight clothing.
3. If the person has heart medicine prescribed for chest pain, help them take it **as their label says.**
4. Stay with them and keep them calm and warm.
5. If the person collapses and does not breathe normally, start **CPR** and use an AED if one is there.

**Do not** let them walk, drive, climb stairs or "work it off".

## If no help is coming

- **Aspirin:** if the person is not allergic to it, has no bleeding problem and has not been told to avoid it, have them **chew** one regular adult aspirin tablet and swallow it. Chewing makes it work faster. Follow the label.
- **Rest is the treatment you have.** Complete rest, sitting propped up, for at least 2 to 3 days. Someone else does everything, including help to the toilet.
- **Check every 15 minutes** at first: pain, breathing, pulse, skin color. Write it down.
- **Getting worse:** pain that will not ease, breathing harder when lying flat, coughing up frothy spit, a very fast, very slow or uneven pulse, gray cold skin, confusion.
- **What you cannot fix:** a blocked artery needs a hospital. Without one, some people recover with rest and some do not. Be ready for CPR, and decide early whether carrying them to help is possible without making them walk.

## Know more

### It may not look like the movies
Many heart attacks start slowly with mild pressure, an ache like heartburn, or only tiredness and shortness of breath. Women, old people and diabetics often have no chest pain at all. If in doubt, treat it as one.

### Things that look like it
Heartburn (burning that rises, worse lying down, eased by an antacid), a pulled chest muscle (hurts when you press the spot or twist), and panic (tingling fingers, fast breathing). Treat as a heart attack until the pain is clearly one of these.

### The weeks after
- Days 1 to 3: rest in bed or a chair.
- Days 4 to 14: short, slow walks on the level, a little longer each day. Stop at any chest pain or breathlessness.
- Weeks 3 to 6: light chores only. No lifting, chopping, hauling or carrying loads.
- Watch for swollen ankles, breathlessness lying flat and waking at night short of breath. These mean the heart is struggling: more rest, propped up, less salt.

### Have ready
Aspirin in every kit, stored dry. Each person's own heart medicines and a written list of them. An AED if the group can afford one.

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".
