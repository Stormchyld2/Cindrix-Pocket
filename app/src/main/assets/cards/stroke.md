Look for **BE FAST**:
- **B** is Balance: sudden loss of balance or dizziness
- **E** is Eyes: sudden trouble seeing
- **F** is Face: one side droops when they smile
- **A** is Arms: one arm drifts down when both are raised
- **S** is Speech: slurred, strange or hard to understand
- **T** is Time: act at once

Other signs: sudden numbness or weakness on one side, sudden confusion, sudden severe headache with no known cause.

1. If phones work, **call 911.** Treatment works best in the first hours.
2. **Note the time** the first sign appeared.
3. Lay the person down with the head and shoulders raised a little. If they are drowsy, lay them on their side with the weak side up.
4. Keep them calm, warm and still.

**Do not** give food, drink or pills, and **do not give aspirin**: some strokes are bleeding in the brain and aspirin makes that worse.

## If no help is coming

- **What you cannot fix:** clot-clearing treatment only exists in a hospital. What you can do is prevent the things that kill stroke patients afterwards: choking, chest infection, falls and pressure sores.
- **Test swallowing before any food or drink.** Sit them fully upright and give one teaspoon of water. If they cough, splutter or their voice goes wet and gurgly, give nothing by mouth yet and try again in a few hours.
- **If swallowing is safe,** give small sips and soft food, sitting upright, with the food placed on the strong side of the mouth.
- **Turn them every 2 hours** if they cannot move themselves. Pad heels, hips and tailbone.
- **Check every hour** for the first day: how awake they are, speech, strength on each side. Getting sleepier or a worse headache with vomiting is a bad sign.
- Some strokes improve a great deal over days and weeks. Do not give up on the person.

## Know more

### Signs that went away
Stroke signs that clear within minutes or an hour are a warning stroke. A full stroke often follows within days. The person rests, avoids effort, and needs a doctor as soon as one can be reached.

### Things that look like a stroke
Low blood sugar in a diabetic (check and treat: see the card "Low blood sugar"), the hour after a seizure, a bad migraine, and a face drooping on one side with no arm or speech trouble. When unsure, treat as a stroke.

### Helping them recover
- Speak slowly in short sentences and give time to answer. Understanding is often better than speech.
- Support the weak arm on a pillow. Never pull on it to lift them.
- Start gentle movement of the weak arm and leg within days, several times a day, so the joints do not stiffen.
- Help them sit, then stand, then walk with a person on the weak side. Falls are the main danger.

### Lowering the risk of another
Rest, no smoking, little salt, and taking any blood pressure medicine they already have exactly as prescribed.
