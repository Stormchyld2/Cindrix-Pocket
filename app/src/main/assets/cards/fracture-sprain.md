Signs: pain, swelling, bruising, a limb bent the wrong way, cannot use or put weight on it.

1. **Do not move** the person if the neck, back or head may be hurt. If phones work, call 911 for a bone through the skin, a deformed limb, heavy bleeding or a hurt neck or back.
2. Stop any bleeding with gentle pressure around, not on, a bone sticking out. Cover an open wound with a clean cloth.
3. **Keep the part still.** Splint it in the position found, with something firm (board, rolled newspaper, sleeping pad) padded with cloth, tied above and below the break.
4. Put a cold pack in a cloth on it for about 20 minutes at a time. Raise it if that does not hurt.
5. **Check beyond the splint:** skin color, warmth, tingling, feeling. If fingers or toes turn pale, blue, cold or numb, loosen the ties.

**Do not** let them test it by walking on it or lifting with it.

## If no help is coming

- **A limb that is cold, blue or numb beyond the break** is losing its blood supply. Pull gently and steadily along the line of the limb and ease it toward its normal shape, then splint. Stop if the pain becomes severe or it will not move. This one step can save the limb.
- **Bone through the skin:** rinse the wound and bone end with at least a quart of clean drinking water, cover with a clean damp dressing, and splint. Infection is the great danger. It needs a surgeon if one can be reached.
- **Splint the joint above and the joint below** the break, pad every hard edge, and recheck fingers or toes every hour on the first day, then twice a day.
- **Raise it and rest it** for the first 3 days to bring the swelling down. Then re-pad and re-tie the splint, which will have gone loose.
- **Pain:** pain reliever by the label, cold packs, and keeping it still.
- **Getting worse:** growing pain inside a splint, numbness, a tight shiny swollen limb, fever, a wound that smells.

## Know more

### Broken, or a sprain
Treat it as broken if it is bent or shortened, you heard a crack, pressing on one spot of bone gives sharp pain, or they cannot take four steps on it. If none of these, it is probably a sprain.

### A sprain
Rest, cold packs, a firm wrap from the toes or fingers upward, and raising it, for 2 days. Then start gentle movement and walking on it as pain allows. A sprain that is kept completely still for weeks heals weak and stiff.

### How long bones take
Fingers and toes 3 to 4 weeks. Collarbone, wrist, forearm and ribs about 6 weeks. Upper arm, ankle and lower leg 8 to 12 weeks. Thigh 3 months or more. Children heal in about half the time. Keep the splint on for the full time, then build strength slowly.

### Thigh and pelvis
Both can bleed enough inside to kill. A broken thigh shortens and swells. Pull gently in line and splint the whole leg against the other leg. For a broken pelvis (pain in the hips or groin after a hard fall or crush, cannot stand), tie a sheet or wide strap tightly around the hips and keep the person flat. Treat for shock. See the card "Shock".

### Collarbone, ribs, fingers and toes
A collarbone needs a sling for 4 to 6 weeks. Broken ribs are left unstrapped: the person must take a deep breath and cough every hour, holding a pillow to the spot, or pneumonia follows. Tape a broken finger or toe to its neighbor with padding between.

### Have ready
A foldable padded splint, elastic wraps, triangular bandages, and tape.

**Train:** Academy course "Casualty care in a crisis".
