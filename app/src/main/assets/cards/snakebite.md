1. Move away from the snake. Do not try to catch or kill it. Note its color and shape if you can do so safely.
2. If phones work, **call 911.** Keep the person calm and still. Walking and panic spread venom faster.
3. Keep the bitten limb still and at about heart level. Take off rings, watches and tight clothing before swelling starts.
4. Wash the bite gently with soap and water. Cover it with a clean, dry cloth.
5. Mark the edge of the swelling with a pen and write the time. Update as it spreads.

**Do not** cut the bite, suck out venom, use a tourniquet, apply ice or electric shock, or give alcohol.

## If no help is coming

- **Antivenom only exists at a hospital.** If one can be reached within hours, go, carrying the person or having them walk slowly if there is no other way.
- **No hospital:** most bites from the pit vipers of West Virginia and Tennessee are survived by healthy adults with rest and care. About one bite in four injects no venom at all.
- **Rest completely for 24 to 48 hours.** Splint the limb like a broken bone, loosely, and keep it at heart level.
- **Drink plenty** of water if not vomiting.
- **Pain:** acetaminophen by the label. Avoid aspirin and ibuprofen-type pain relievers, since venom already thins the blood.
- **Check every hour** at first: how far the swelling has moved past your pen marks, bleeding from the gums or nose, breathing, alertness.
- **Getting worse:** swelling passing a major joint and still moving, bleeding from the gums or in the urine, vomiting, fainting, trouble breathing.
- **The days after:** blood-filled blisters and dark skin near the bite are common. Leave blisters whole, keep it clean and covered, and watch for infection.

## Know more

### Which snakes
In West Virginia and Tennessee the venomous snakes are the **timber rattlesnake** and the **copperhead**, plus the **cottonmouth** and the **pygmy rattlesnake** in parts of Tennessee. All are pit vipers: thick body, wide triangular head, a pit between eye and nostril. Their venom damages tissue and blood. Copperhead bites are painful but rarely kill.

### Was venom injected
Venom shows within 30 to 60 minutes: burning pain, swelling that spreads, bruising. Fang marks with no pain or swelling after 8 hours means a dry bite. Clean it and watch for infection.

### Children and small adults
The same venom in a smaller body does more harm. Treat any bite on a child as severe.

### Not being bitten
Most bites are on hands and ankles. Wear boots and long trousers, step onto logs rather than over them, do not reach where you cannot see, use a light at night, and leave every snake alone. A dead snake can still bite by reflex for an hour.
