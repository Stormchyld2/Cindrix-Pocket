**Dispose of a dead farm animal within 48 hours and never put it where it can reach water.** If several animals die together or you suspect a disease, keep people and animals away and call 911 only for a danger to people; call the vet or the State Veterinarian for the animals.

1. **Wear gloves and boots.** Do not butcher or eat a sick or unexplained dead animal.
2. **Move it only as far as needed,** away from wells, streams, ponds and the house.
3. **Pick the method:** pick-up or rendering, a landfill, **composting**, then burial.
4. **Compost:** firm, flat, well-drained pad, under a roof or 6-mil plastic. A foot of sawdust, wood chips or straw below, the animal on top, a foot of carbon over every side. Keep it damp. It should reach 130 to 150 °F; turn it when it peaks.
5. **Bury only at a site that passes every check:** more than 300 feet from a well, more than 165 feet from property lines, more than 100 feet from streams and ditches, at least 2 feet above rock and the high water table, in soil that drains slowly. At least 2 feet of soil on top. Mound it.
6. **Wash hands and tools,** change clothes, and write the record.

**Do not** dump it in a sinkhole, a creek, a gully or a pond, and **do not** bury on limestone ground you have not checked.

## If no help is coming

- **Over 10,000 pounds** lost at once: tell the State Veterinarian. Landfill, composting or rendering may be the only practical ways.
- **Coyotes or dogs digging:** more soil or a fence.
- **Frozen or rocky ground:** compost above ground instead of digging.
- **Disease suspected:** do not move parts around; use a vet, rendering or deep burial at a verified site.
- **Keep records:** species, number, date, method, place.

## Know more

### Karst
On limestone ground with sinkholes, the distances above are not enough. Compost above ground, or take the animal to a landfill or renderer.

### Cattle over 30 months
Renderers may refuse them. Ask first.

### Have ready
Gloves, boots, shovel, carbon material (sawdust, chips, straw), 6-mil plastic, a thermometer, a notebook.

**Train:** the plan "Remains management and water protection (SOP)".

Source: Tennessee Department of Agriculture policy on disposal of dead farm animals; USDA-NRCS Tennessee guide sheet TN316 and standards 316 and 317; USDA APHIS Emergency Carcass Management Desk Reference Guide.
