1. **Baby under 3 months with a temperature of 100.4 F (38 C) or higher: get to a doctor or emergency room right away,** day or night, if one can be reached.
2. For an older child, take the temperature and note it. Dress them lightly, keep the room comfortable, and offer fluids often.
3. **Follow the label on any medicine.** Never give aspirin to a child or teenager.
4. Watch the child more than the number. Drinking, playing and waking up well are good signs.

**It is an emergency** (call 911 if phones work) for trouble breathing, a stiff neck, a rash that does not fade when pressed, a seizure, a child who is hard to wake, limp, very irritable, will not drink, or has not passed urine in 8 hours.

**Do not** bundle them up, or use cold baths or alcohol rubs.

## If no help is coming

- **Fluids come first.** Offer a sip or a spoonful every few minutes. Breast milk, rehydration drink, water, thin soup. Count wet diapers or trips to pass urine: fewer than 3 in a day is too few.
- **Cool for comfort:** one light layer, a damp cloth on the forehead, a lukewarm (not cold) sponge.
- **Find where the infection is:** ear (pulling at it), throat, chest (fast breathing, cough), urine (crying when passing it), skin, belly.
- **Check every 2 to 3 hours,** day and night: breathing, color, how they respond to you, drinking.
- **Getting worse:** breathing fast with the ribs pulling in, grunting, a weak cry, limp, sunken eyes or soft spot, cold hands and feet with a hot body, a spotted rash that does not fade under a pressed glass.
- **Getting better:** playing a little between fevers, drinking, wet diapers.
- **A baby under 3 months with a fever** can become very ill very fast. It is a strong reason to travel to care.

## Know more

### How fast is too fast to breathe
Count breaths for a full minute while the child is calm. Too fast is more than 60 in a baby under 2 months, more than 50 from 2 months to 1 year, more than 40 from 1 to 5 years.

### A seizure with fever
Common between 6 months and 5 years. Lay the child on their side, protect the head, note the time. Most stop in under 5 minutes and the child recovers. See the card "Seizure".

### Rashes
Press a clear glass on the rash. Spots that fade are usually a harmless virus rash. Dark red or purple spots that **do not fade** are an emergency.

### Teething and vaccines
Teething causes only a slight rise. A mild fever for a day or two after a vaccine is expected.

### Have ready
A thermometer, a children's fever reducer with its measuring syringe and label, rehydration salts, and a notebook.
