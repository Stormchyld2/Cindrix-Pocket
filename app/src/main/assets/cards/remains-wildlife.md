**Do not touch a dead wild animal with bare hands, and keep game scraps away from water.** If someone is bitten or scratched, wash well and call 911 or a doctor.

1. **Wear gloves** and use a shovel. Keep dogs and children away. Wash hands after.
2. **One or two dead animals on open land** can usually be left alone, well away from water and paths.
3. **Move it** if it is in your well area, near the house, or in a water source: wrap, carry, and put it far from water.
4. **Many dead birds or animals at once** may mean disease or poison. Do not touch them. Keep pets and livestock away. Note the date and place, and tell the wildlife agency or a vet if you can.
5. **Game scraps** (offal, hide, bones): bury at least 2 feet deep, well away from water, or compost with carbon, or leave far from camp for scavengers. Never put them in a stream, sinkhole or near a well.

**Do not** handle an animal that acted strangely before it died.

## If no help is coming

- **Deer in a chronic wasting disease area:** check the state wildlife agency rules before moving a carcass or its parts.
- **Wash and treat water** as usual. Wash tools and boots.
- **Keep records** of large die-offs: date, place, species, number.

## Know more

### Why gloves
Wild animals can carry rabies, tularemia, avian flu and other diseases.

### Have ready
Gloves, shovel, soap and water, carbon material, a notebook.

**Train:** the plan "Remains management and water protection (SOP)".

Source: CDC and USDA APHIS public guidance on handling dead wildlife; Tennessee Department of Agriculture and TDEC guidance on carcass disposal.

Reference only, not medical or veterinary advice. Where help can be reached, call 911.
