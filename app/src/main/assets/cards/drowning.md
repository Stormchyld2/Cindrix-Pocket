**Reach or throw. Don't go.**

1. Shout for help. If phones work, call 911.
2. From shore, **reach** with a pole or branch, or **throw** something that floats (a cooler, a ring, a rope). Do not swim out unless you are trained.
3. Once the person is out, check for breathing. If they do not respond and are not breathing normally, **start CPR, beginning with 2 breaths**. Drowning is lack of air, so the breaths matter most.
4. Roll them onto their side if they vomit.
5. Take off wet clothes and wrap them in dry things.

**Do not** try to drain water from the lungs, press on the belly, or jump in after them without something that floats.

## If no help is coming

- **Keep CPR going longer than usual,** up to an hour or more, if the water was cold. People have recovered after long periods under cold water, children especially.
- **Anyone who breathed in water is watched for 24 hours,** even if they seem fine. The lungs can fill with fluid hours later.
- **Check every 30 minutes** for the first 8 hours: how fast they breathe, any cough, color of the lips, how awake they are.
- **Getting worse:** a cough that will not stop, fast or hard breathing, chest pain, blue lips, unusual sleepiness, frothy spit. Sit them upright and keep them resting. This needs a hospital if one can be reached.
- **Warm them slowly.** Cold water chills fast. See the card "Hypothermia and frostbite".

## Know more

### What drowning looks like
It is quiet. The person cannot shout or wave. The head is low with the mouth at the surface, the body upright, the arms pressing down on the water, and there is no kicking. It lasts under a minute before they go under.

### Getting a person out
Go only if you must and only with something that floats to push at them, so they grab it and not you. Approach from behind. In a river, throw a rope and swing them to the bank downstream. Never tie a rope around yourself in moving water.

### Diving and falls into water
Suspect a neck injury. Float them face up, hold the head in line with the body, and slide them out on a board if you can. See the card "Neck or back injury".

### Ice
Lie flat to spread your weight and reach with a branch, ladder or rope from solid ground. A person pulled from ice water must be handled gently and kept lying down.

### Keeping it from happening
Children near water are watched by an adult doing nothing else. Everyone in a boat wears a life jacket. Never wade a fast river above knee depth: see the card "Crossing water safely".

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".
