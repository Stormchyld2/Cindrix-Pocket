**You need three things: heat, fuel and air.** Prepare **everything before you light.**
1. **Pick the spot:** sheltered, on bare ground or stones, away from overhanging branches. Clear a circle at least 3 feet (1 m) wide.
2. **Tinder:** very dry, fine stuff: dry grass, birch bark, cotton fluff, pine needles, fuzz sticks.
3. **Kindling:** pencil-thin sticks, then thumb-thick ones. Gather **much more than you think.**
4. **Fuel:** wrist-thick wood and bigger. **Dead wood that snaps** is dry.
5. **Build:** tipi or log cabin style. **Leave gaps for air.**
6. **Light the tinder** from the upwind side. Add thin kindling slowly.
7. **In rain:** split wet wood to use the dry center, build on a base of sticks, and shield the fire.
8. **Never leave a fire unattended.** Put it out with water, stir the ashes and feel that they are cold.
9. **Never burn indoors** or in a tent. Carbon monoxide kills.

## If you have less gear

- **No lighter:** a ferro rod and knife, a magnifying glass or lens in strong sun, or a battery and steel wool.
- **Friction fires** (bow drill) work but take practice. Learn before you need it.
- **Wet everything:** look for standing dead wood, the dry heart of split logs, and resin-rich pine.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Too little kindling, no air, wet wood, and building on wet or snowy ground.

### Practice
Light a fire in the rain once. It teaches more than ten dry ones.

### Fire safety
Water and a shovel at hand. See "Making water safe: boil, filter, treat" for boiling.

**Train:** Academy course "First 72 hours".
