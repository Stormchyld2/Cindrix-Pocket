Plan to travel in daylight. If you must move at night, slow down and make it easy.
1. **Wait for first light** if you can.
2. **Let your eyes adjust** for about 30 minutes. White light ruins it in a second. Use a **red light** if you have one.
3. **Look slightly to the side** of what you want to see. Dim things show better at the edge of your sight.
4. **Pick routes with handrails:** a road, a fence line, a stream or a ridge you can follow by feel or sound.
5. **Know what tells you to stop** (a road, a river, a change of slope).
6. **Count your paces** and hold your bearing. Distances feel longer at night.
7. **Walk slowly,** feet first on the ground before you put weight down, hands in front.
8. **Stop and rest** if unsure.

## If you have less gear

- **No red light:** red cloth or tape over a flashlight.
- **No light:** wait for the moon or dawn. Move by feel and sound along a handrail.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
White light, rushing, no handrail, and walking on after a doubt.

### Practice
Walk a known route at night with a friend before you need it.

**Train:** Academy course "Land navigation".

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on night movement, paraphrased.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
