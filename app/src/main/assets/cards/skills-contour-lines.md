Contour lines join points of the same height. They tell you how steep the ground is before you walk on it.
1. **Find the contour interval** in the map margin. Every line is that much higher or lower than its neighbor.
2. **Bold lines** are numbered with their height. Count up or down from them.
3. **Lines close together** mean a steep slope. **Far apart** means gentle ground.
4. **Closed rings** mean a hilltop (heights rise toward the center) or a pit (tick marks pointing inward).
5. **A ridge:** lines bend in a U or V pointing **down** the slope.
6. **A valley or stream:** lines bend in a V pointing **up** the slope. Water runs out the open end.
7. **A saddle** is a low spot between two high points.
8. **Use it:** a ridge keeps you high and dry, a valley leads to water but is wet and slow, and many close lines mean a hard climb.

## If you have less gear

- **No map:** read the land itself. Water runs down, valleys lead to streams, ridges give views.
- **Make a quick sketch** of what you see and mark the high ground and water.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Mixing up a ridge and a valley. Ignoring the interval. Not matching the map to the real ground.

### Practice
Hike with a map and name each slope and stream before you reach it.

**Train:** Academy course "Land navigation".
