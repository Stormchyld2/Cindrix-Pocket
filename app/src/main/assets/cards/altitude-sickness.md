Signs at 8,000 feet (2,400 m) and above: headache, nausea, dizziness, poor sleep, feeling very tired.

1. **Stop climbing.** Rest. Drink water and eat.
2. **Do not go higher until the signs are gone.**
3. If you do not get better in a day, or get worse, **go down at least 1,000 to 2,000 feet (300 to 600 m)**.
4. Avoid alcohol, sleeping pills and heavy effort in the first days at height.

**Go down now** (call 911 if phones work) for a cough with breathlessness at rest, bubbling in the chest, confusion, stumbling, or a headache that will not ease. These can kill within hours.

## If no help is coming

- **Going down is the cure, and you can do it yourself.** Do not wait for morning if the danger signs are present. Carry the person or support them: effort makes it worse.
- **The walking test:** have them walk heel to toe along a straight line. Stumbling like a drunk means the brain is swelling. Go down at once.
- **The breathing test:** breathless while sitting still, a wet cough, or pink frothy spit means fluid in the lungs. Keep them sitting upright and warm, and go down at once.
- **Never leave a sick person alone,** and never send them down alone.
- **Getting better** usually shows within hours of losing height. Rest for 2 to 3 days before trying to go up again, and go slower.

## Know more

### In West Virginia and Tennessee
The highest ground in both states is under 7,000 feet, so this illness is not expected at home. It matters if the group travels to the Rockies or other high ranges.

### Preventing it
Above 8,000 feet, raise your sleeping height by no more than about 1,500 feet a day, and take a rest day every third day. Climb high in the day and sleep low. Drink enough to keep urine pale.

### What it is not
Headache and tiredness on a hot day at low height is heat or lack of water. Breathlessness on effort that eases with rest is normal at height.
