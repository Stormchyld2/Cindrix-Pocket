1. **Call 911 or Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) right away** if you think a child swallowed a button battery or a magnet, even if they seem fine.
2. Do not make the person vomit. Nothing to eat or drink if a battery was swallowed.
3. Note what was swallowed, when, and the size. Keep the packaging or a matching item.
4. If the person is choking, see "Choking: adult or child".

## If no help is coming

- **A button battery burns the throat in 2 hours.** Get to care by the fastest way.
- **If the person is over 12 months and the battery was swallowed in the last 12 hours,** a small amount of honey (a teaspoon or two) every 10 minutes, up to 6 doses, may slow burns. Do not give honey to a baby under 12 months.
- **Magnets:** more than one can join through the bowel wall. Treat as an emergency.
- **Coins and small smooth objects** often pass on their own in days. Check the stool. Watch for drooling, vomiting, pain, cough or refusing food.
- **Sharp objects** need care. Do not wait.
- **No scan or hospital:** keep the person upright, calm, and moving toward care while you can.

## Know more

### Prevention
Keep batteries, magnets and coins in sealed, high places. Check remotes and toys for loose battery covers.

### Look for hidden batteries
In remotes, car fobs, hearing aids, lights and greeting cards.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".
