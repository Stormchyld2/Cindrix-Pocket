**Do not choose a site from a distance alone: check the soil, how deep the water is, where water drains, and the rules first.** If someone is hurt, call 911.

1. **Paper check:** soil survey (depth to rock and to the high water table, flooding, how fast water moves through), geology map (limestone?), and your sketch of every well, spring, creek, ditch and low spot, including neighbours and downhill.
2. **Karst check:** sinkholes or dips with no outlet, vanishing streams, springs, caves, bare limestone, thin soil. Any of these: **do not bury there.**
3. **Field check:** dig a test hole to the depth you would bury. Gravel, sand, cracked or very shallow soil: no. Mottled or grey soil, seeps or standing water: no.
4. **Flow check:** rain must run away from the site, never into it. No floodplains, no ponding, no spots upslope of a well or spring.
5. **Utilities check:** pipes, drain tiles, septic fields, old graves.
6. **Rules check:** county zoning and health department, and state rules. Write down who you asked.
7. **Minimums as a floor, not a clearance:** people (WHO field manual), 5 to 10 feet deep with 3 to 4 feet of soil on top, 100 feet from springs and streams and 650 feet from any well or drinking water, and at least 4 feet (5 in sand) between the bottom and the highest the water rises; livestock, more than 300 feet from a well, 165 feet from property lines, 100 feet from water, 2 feet above rock and the water table.
8. **Record it** on the site sheet: date, who checked, soil, depths, slope, karst signs, distances, rules.

**Do not** bury in, beside or above a sinkhole, cave, spring or losing stream, and **do not** fill sinkholes.

## If no help is coming

- **Any doubt:** pick another site or method. Cold storage for a short time, composting above ground (animals), crematory, landfill or renderer if working.
- **In limestone country,** assume water moves fast and far underground. Treat all water, always.
- **Ask now, while you can:** county Extension, NRCS field office, county health department, and the oldest local people (where the ground has opened, where creeks vanish, where springs run). **Do this before you need it.**
- **When no one can be asked:** walk the land after heavy rain and watch where water goes; test-hole to 6 feet in the wet season; pick high, deep, even soil; treat any doubt as a no; and treat all your water.

## Know more

### Why distance alone fails
In karst, water can drop into a sinkhole and come out at a spring or well far away, quickly and unfiltered. A measured distance on the surface does not predict where it goes.

### Print before you need it
Print the soil and geology maps for your land while the internet works.

### Have ready
Probe rod, shovel, tape, notebook, printed maps, a camera.

**Train:** the plan "Remains management and water protection (SOP)".

Source: WHO, PAHO, ICRC and IFRC, Management of Dead Bodies after Disasters (2nd edition, 2016), which also says to seek a geological and hydrological opinion before opening a burial ground; Tennessee Department of Agriculture policy and USDA-NRCS Tennessee guide sheet TN316; USGS karst and ground-water publications for Tennessee.
