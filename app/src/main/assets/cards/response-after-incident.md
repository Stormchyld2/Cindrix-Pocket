**Make sure everyone is safe first. If phones work, call 911 for anyone who needs help.**
1. **Count everyone.** Say each person's name. Anyone missing or hurt comes first.
2. **Give first aid** with the matching card.
3. **Secure the site** only as far as is safe: gates closed, doors locked, nobody left alone to watch.
4. **Write down what happened** while it is fresh: who saw what, when, in order. Mark what you saw and what someone told you.

## If no help is coming

- **Nobody is coming to take over,** so close the incident yourselves. Set the posture to match how things stand and close it only when you can say why it is safe.
- **Keep things as they are** if police may come later. Do not clean up what matters.
- **Look after each other.** Shock, fear and anger are normal. Eat, drink, rest, and talk it through. See "Panic, fear and overwhelming stress".
- **Check for injuries again** in a few hours. Some show up late.
- **Restock and repair:** replace what you used, fix what broke, and rest the watch.
- **Review within a few days:** what worked, what did not, and what to change in the plan, the watch and the gear.

## Know more

### The written account
Time, place, who, what, what you did. Keep it plain and in order. It protects people later.

### After a hard event
Talk, sleep and routine help most. Watch for people who go quiet.

**Train:** Academy course "Operator emergency management".

Source: Cindrix starter procedure (an example for your family to edit), built on Ready.gov and FEMA emergency-planning guidance on family communication and recovery, and general after-action practice.
