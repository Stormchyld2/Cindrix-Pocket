Signs: pounding heart, shaking, tight chest, dizzy, a feeling of unreality, cannot think straight, cannot sleep.

1. Stop and sit somewhere safe. Tell yourself, "This feeling will pass."
2. Breathe **in for 4, out for 6**, for a few minutes.
3. Name **5 things you see, 4 you touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.**
4. Drink water, eat, and rest when you can.

**If you or someone near you is thinking of suicide, call or text 988** (United States), any time. If anyone is in immediate danger, **call 911**.

## If no help is coming

- **Keep a simple routine:** wake, eat, work, wash, sleep at set times. Routine steadies the mind.
- **Give everyone a job,** children included. Useful work lowers fear.
- **Talk it out** with someone you trust, in a short daily check-in. Say what is hard.
- **Sleep:** protect it. Take turns on watch so each person gets a long block.
- **Limit bad news.** Check the radio at set times, not all day.
- **Watch each other.** Withdrawal, not eating, or talk of giving up needs a gentle direct talk. See "Someone is thinking of suicide".

## Know more

### Normal reactions
After a shock, many people feel numb, angry, tearful or restless. These usually ease in days to weeks.

### When it is more
Weeks of not eating, not sleeping, or hopeless thoughts need care and company.

### Children
Keep to routines, tell them the truth in simple words, and stay close.

**Train:** Academy course "Survival mindset".

Source: SAMHSA, NIMH and Red Cross psychological first aid.
