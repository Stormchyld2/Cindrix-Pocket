1. **Do not pull the object out.** It may be plugging a blood vessel.
2. **If phones work, call 911.**
3. Keep the person still. Put on gloves if you can.
4. **Pad around the object** with thick cloth or rolled gauze, and tape the padding in place without pressing on the object.
5. If it is bleeding, press firmly **around** the object, never on it.

## If no help is coming

- **Leave it in** in the chest, belly, neck, eye or head. Only a doctor should remove it.
- **Keep the person lying down and warm** to slow shock. See "Shock". No food or drink.
- **Shorten a long object** only if you must move the person: stabilize it, then cut it, with someone holding it still.
- **A small shallow splinter or thorn** can be removed. See "Splinters and thorns".
- **Move toward care** by any means, keeping the object steady the whole way.

## Know more

### Why leave it in
It acts as a plug. Removing it can start heavy bleeding that you cannot control.

### Tetanus
Any deep puncture needs tetanus checked when a clinic is reachable.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".

Source: American Red Cross and Mayo Clinic first aid guidance.
