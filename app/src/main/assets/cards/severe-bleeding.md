**Stop the blood first.** If phones work, call 911 or have someone call while you work.

1. Find where the blood is coming from. Cut or pull clothing away so you can see it.
2. **Press hard** straight on the wound with a clean cloth, using both hands. Do not let up.
3. If the wound is deep or gaping, **pack it**: push clean cloth or gauze into the wound until it is full, then keep pressing.
4. If blood soaks through, **add more cloth on top**. Do not take the first layer off.
5. Arm or leg bleeding that will not stop: put a tourniquet **2 to 3 inches above the wound** (not over a joint), tighten until the bleeding stops, and write down the time.
6. Keep the person lying down and warm.

**Watch for shock:** pale, cold, sweaty skin; fast, weak pulse; fast breathing; confusion; strong thirst.

**Do not:**
- Lift the cloth to look. Hold pressure for 10 full minutes by the clock.
- Pull out an object stuck deep in the wound. Pad around it.
- Put a tourniquet on the neck, chest or belly.

## If no help is coming

- **Tourniquet on under 2 hours, person not in shock:** pack the wound, wrap a tight pressure bandage over it, then loosen the tourniquet slowly while watching. If bleeding starts again, tighten it and leave it.
- **Tourniquet on over 6 hours:** leave it on. Taking it off now is a job for a medical professional.
- **Shock:** keep them flat and warm. If they are awake and can swallow, give small sips of water. See the card "Shock".
- **Check every 15 minutes** for the first hours: is the bandage soaking through, are they awake, is the skin beyond the bandage warm and pink.
- **Decide early** whether you can carry the person to help. A wound that needed a tourniquet needs a surgeon.

## Know more

### No tourniquet on hand
1. Use a band of cloth **at least 2 inches wide**: a folded triangular bandage, a strip of shirt, a wide strap. Thin cord, wire or a narrow belt cuts in and usually fails.
2. Wrap it around the limb 2 to 3 inches above the wound and tie a half knot.
3. Lay a strong stick on the knot and tie a full knot over it.
4. **Twist the stick** until the bleeding stops. It will hurt. That is expected.
5. Tie the stick in place so it cannot unwind, and write down the time.

### Wound in the groin, armpit or neck
A tourniquet cannot go here. Pack the wound tightly with gauze or clean cloth, pushing it in toward where the blood comes from, then press down with both hands or a knee and hold for at least 10 minutes. On the neck, press on the wound only. Never squeeze both sides of the neck or press on the windpipe.

### The tourniquet is on and it still bleeds
Tighten it more. If it still bleeds, put a **second tourniquet right beside the first**, on the side nearer the body. Do not take the first one off.

### Clotting gauze
If you stock gauze made to speed clotting, pack it into the wound first, right against the bleeding point, then press for the time printed on the packet (usually 3 minutes). Follow the packet.

### Bleeding you cannot see
After a hard blow or fall, suspect bleeding inside if there are shock signs with little blood showing, a hard, swollen or very tender belly, bruising across the belly or flank, or blood coughed up or vomited. You cannot stop this in the field. Keep the person still, flat and warm, give nothing but small sips, and get them to a surgeon if there is any way to.

### Children, and people on blood thinners
The steps are the same. A child has far less blood to lose, so act faster and treat any soaked bandage as serious. A person on blood-thinning medicine bleeds longer: hold pressure for 20 minutes before you check.

### The days after
- Leave the packing in for the first day. After that, wet it with clean drinking water and ease it out slowly, ready to pack again if bleeding starts.
- Rinse the wound with plenty of clean drinking water. Do not sew or tape a deep or dirty wound shut. Cover it and let it heal from the inside.
- Change the dressing every day with clean hands. Look for spreading redness, heat, swelling, pus, red streaks or fever. See the cards "Cuts, scrapes and bites" and "Signs a wound is infected".
- A deep or dirty wound needs a tetanus booster when care can be reached, if the last one was over 5 years ago.

### Have ready
A proper tourniquet for each adult's kit, rolled gauze, a pressure bandage, gloves, strong scissors and a marker for writing the time.

**Train:** Academy courses "Stop the Bleed" and "Casualty care in a crisis".

Source: Stop the Bleed (American College of Surgeons); Committee on Tactical Combat Casualty Care guidelines; Wilderness Medical Society; Red Cross.
