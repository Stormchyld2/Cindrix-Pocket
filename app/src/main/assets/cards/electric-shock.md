1. **Do not touch the person** while they may still be in contact with the electricity.
2. **Turn off the power** at the switch or breaker if you can. If you cannot, use a dry object that does not conduct, such as dry wood, cardboard or plastic, to move the source away.
3. **Stay at least 20 feet (6 metres) away from downed high-voltage wires.** Farther if they are sparking.
4. If the person is not breathing or moving, **start CPR** (see the CPR cards).
5. Do not move the person unless there is danger, since the neck or back may be hurt.
6. Cover burns with a clean, non-fluffy cloth.
7. Keep the person warm. If phones work, call 911 for any shock that caused burns, confusion, trouble breathing, a blackout, or that came from high voltage or lightning.

**Do not** use anything wet or metal to reach them.

## If no help is coming

- **The heart is the first danger.** The heartbeat can go wrong for hours afterward. The person rests completely for 24 hours and is never alone. Be ready for CPR.
- **Check every 30 minutes** for the first 6 hours: pulse (is it even?), breathing, alertness.
- **The burns are deeper than they look.** Electricity cooks tissue under the skin between the place it went in and the place it came out. Look for both marks. Treat them as deep burns: see the card "Burns".
- **Drink plenty.** Damaged muscle clogs the kidneys. Dark brown or red urine is the warning: push fluids until it runs pale.
- **Watch the limb** between the two marks for swelling, tightness, growing pain and numbness. That needs a surgeon.
- **Getting better:** steady pulse, clear urine, pain easing over a day.

## Know more

### Lightning
The person carries no charge and is safe to touch. The heart often restarts by itself but breathing does not, so give breaths even with a pulse. Expect burns, ringing ears, confusion, and weak, cold, blue legs for some hours. See the card "Lightning".

### Low voltage at home or camp
A shock from a battery bank, inverter or generator can still stop the heart, especially with wet hands or across the chest. If the person blacked out, has chest pain or an uneven pulse, treat as serious.

### Falls
Many shock injuries are from the fall that follows. Check the head, neck and limbs.

### Children
A child who bit a live cable can bleed heavily from the lip days later when the scab comes away. Press on it firmly for 10 minutes.

### Keeping it from happening
Turn off and unplug before any repair. Keep batteries, inverters and generators dry and off the ground. Use one hand when working near live parts. Treat every downed wire as live.

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".
