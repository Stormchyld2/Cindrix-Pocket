A rally point is a place everyone knows to go if you are separated or must leave in a hurry.
1. **A first place close to home,** such as a mailbox, tree or neighbor's gate, safe and easy to describe.
2. **A second place away from your street,** such as a school, church, library or park.
3. **A third place further out,** in another direction, for a wider emergency.
4. **Write each one down** with its address or grid reference and how to get there on foot.
5. **Walk the routes together,** and note a second route to each.
6. **Agree how long to wait** (for example, 30 minutes at the first, then the second) and leave a note saying where you went.
7. **Choose an out-of-area contact** everyone can call or text.
8. **Check it twice a year** and when someone moves house or changes school or work.

## If you have less gear

- **No phone:** a note left at the first place and a fixed meeting time at the second.
- **No map:** use landmarks everyone knows.
- **If you cannot reach any:** go to the highest or farthest one you can safely reach.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Picking places no one can find in the dark, and not telling the children.

### Practice
Walk the route once a year. Time it.

**Train:** Academy course "Operator emergency management".

Source: Ready.gov family communication plan and US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) on rally points, paraphrased.
