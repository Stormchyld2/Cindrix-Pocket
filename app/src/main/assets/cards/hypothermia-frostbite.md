**Hypothermia** (the body core too cold): shivering, tired, confused, clumsy, slurred speech, drowsy. In a baby: cold bright-red skin and very low energy. **When shivering stops and the person is very sleepy, it is severe.** If phones work, call 911.

1. Move to shelter. **Handle gently and keep them lying flat.**
2. Take off wet clothing. Wrap in dry blankets, coats or a sleeping bag, with something under them.
3. **Warm the chest, neck and head first**, not the arms and legs. Warm water bottles wrapped in cloth at the chest and armpits, or another person's body heat.
4. If awake and able to swallow, give warm sweet drinks.

**Frostbite:** skin numb, white, gray-yellow or waxy, hard or stiff. Get warm, do not rub, and do not walk on frostbitten feet if you can avoid it.

**Do not** give alcohol, rub the skin, or put the person in a hot bath or against a fire.

## If no help is coming

- **Mild (shivering, awake, makes sense):** shelter, dry clothes, food and warm sweet drinks, and gentle movement. They will warm up over an hour or two.
- **Severe (not shivering, confused or unconscious):** build a wrap. Lay down a pad, then a sleeping bag or blankets, the person in dry clothes, wrapped warm bottles at the chest and armpits, more blankets, then a tarp or plastic sheet around the whole bundle. Keep them flat and still. Rough handling can stop a cold heart.
- **Warming takes many hours.** Refill the warm bottles and keep at it. Check breathing and pulse for a full minute before deciding there is none.
- **No pulse:** start CPR and keep warming. A cold person is not dead until they are warm and dead. People have recovered after hours.
- **Afterward:** rest for a day, eat well, stay warm. They chill again easily for a while.

## Know more

### Thawing frostbite
Thaw only if it cannot freeze again and the person will not have to walk on it. Refrozen tissue is destroyed.
1. Warm it in water that feels comfortably warm to an unhurt elbow, about body temperature. Keep adding warm water. It takes 15 to 30 minutes, until the part is soft and red or purple.
2. It will hurt badly. Give pain reliever by the label. An ibuprofen-type pain reliever also protects the tissue.
3. Dry gently. Put clean dry cloth between the toes or fingers. Leave blisters whole. Raise the part.
4. Protect it from any pressure or cold. It heals over weeks. Blackened tissue separates by itself over a month or more: do not cut it away early.

### Frostnip
Pale numb skin on the nose, ears, cheeks or fingers that is still soft. Warm it against bare skin (fingers in the armpits). It recovers fully.

### Cold, wet feet
Feet kept cold and wet for days go numb, swollen and pale, then painful. Dry them, warm them slowly in the air, raise them, and change into dry socks twice a day.

### Seeing it coming
Watch each other for the "umbles": stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles. Wet clothing, wind, tiredness and hunger are the causes. Stop early, get dry, eat, and shelter. See the card "Staying warm: C.O.L.D.".
