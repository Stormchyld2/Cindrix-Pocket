A six-digit grid reference names a spot to within about 100 meters.
1. **Find the grid lines** on your map. They are numbered along the edges.
2. **Read right, then up.** First the vertical line just left of the spot (easting), then the horizontal line just below it (northing).
3. **Use two digits** of the line number, then estimate **a third** for how far across the square, in tenths (0 to 9).
4. **Example:** vertical line 43, six tenths of the way to 44: 436. Horizontal line 77, two tenths up: 772. The reference is **436772**.
5. **Say it clearly,** in two groups of three: "four three six, seven seven two".
6. **To find a place,** go along the bottom to the first three digits, then up to the last three.
7. **Check twice.** The common mistake is reading up before right.

## If you have less gear

- **No plotting card:** the corner of a compass or a ruler marked in tenths.
- **Cut a card** with ten marks along the edge and use it as your own.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Reading the numbers in the wrong order, and estimating a tenth wildly.

### Practice
Pick ten spots on a map, write their references, and have someone else find them.

**Train:** Academy course "Land navigation".

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on grid coordinates, paraphrased.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
