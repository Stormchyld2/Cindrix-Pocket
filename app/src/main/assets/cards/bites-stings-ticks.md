**Snake bite:** move away from the snake, keep the person calm and the limb still, take off rings, and see the card "Snakebite". **Do not** cut, suck, ice or use a tourniquet.

**Bee or wasp sting:**
1. Scrape the stinger out sideways with a fingernail or card edge.
2. Wash, then hold a cold wet cloth on it.
3. **Watch for a severe allergic reaction** for 30 minutes: swelling of the face or tongue, trouble breathing, faintness. See the card "Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)". If phones work, call 911 for these signs.

**Tick:**
1. Use fine tweezers close to the skin. Pull straight up, slowly and steadily.
2. Clean the bite with soap and water.
3. Write down the date and where on the body.

## If no help is coming

- **Many stings at once** (a nest disturbed): get away first, then scrape out stingers. Hundreds of stings can poison an adult by venom alone, and far fewer can harm a child. Treat 30 or more stings on a child, or 100 or more on an adult, as dangerous. Rest, plenty to drink, and watch for vomiting, dark urine and weakness over the next day.
- **Swelling from a sting** that keeps growing for 2 days is a large local reaction, not an infection. Raise the limb and use cold cloths.
- **After a tick,** watch for 30 days. A spreading ring-shaped rash, fever, bad headache, aching joints, or a spotted rash that starts at the wrists and ankles means a tick-borne illness. Those need antibiotics from a doctor, and one of them (spotted fever) can kill if left. This is a reason to travel to care.
- **Any bite that breaks the skin** can get infected. Wash it hard with soap and plenty of water. See the card "Cuts, scrapes and bites".

## Know more

### Spiders
- **Black widow** (shiny black, red hourglass underneath): sharp bite, then cramps in the belly, back and chest within hours, with sweating. Painful but rarely fatal in adults. Rest, warmth on the cramping muscles, pain reliever by the label. Children and old people are at more risk.
- **Brown recluse** (brown, violin mark, found in Tennessee): little pain at first, then a blister that turns into a dark sore over days. Keep it clean and covered. It heals slowly over weeks.

### Getting the whole tick
Do not burn it, smother it with grease or twist it. If the mouth parts stay in, leave them: the skin pushes them out. Check the whole body every evening, especially hairline, ears, armpits, waist, groin and behind the knees. A tick taken off within a day rarely passes on disease.

### Animal and human bites
These carry a high risk of infection, and wild animal bites carry rabies. See the card "Cuts, scrapes and bites".

### Keeping them off
Long sleeves, trousers tucked into socks, light colors so ticks show, and repellent on clothing. Shake out boots and bedding. Keep food covered so wasps stay away.
