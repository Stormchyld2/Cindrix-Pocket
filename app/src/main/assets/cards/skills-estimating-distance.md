Rough numbers are good enough. Check them against something you can measure.
1. **Lightning and thunder:** count seconds from flash to bang. About 3 seconds is 1 kilometer; about 5 seconds is 1 mile.
2. **Daylight left:** hold your arm out and stack your fingers sideways between the horizon and the sun. Each finger is about 15 minutes and a fist about an hour.
3. **Measure 100 meters** once in a walk, then picture that length to judge other distances.
4. **Details you can see:** at about 200 meters you see faces and clothing; at 500 meters details are lost; at 1 kilometer people are small shapes.
5. **Pace count** gives accurate distance on the ground. See "Measuring distance by pace count".
6. **Expect bias.** Distances look shorter across water, snow or clear air, and longer in rough ground or haze.
7. **On a map,** measure along your route with a string, then hold it against the scale bar.

## If you have less gear

- **No watch:** sun fingers give time. The sun moves about 15 degrees an hour.
- **No tape:** your pace, a rope of known length, or a stick cut to a known length.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Judging long distances by eye, and forgetting uphill takes longer.

### Practice
Guess a distance, then pace it. Repeat until your guess is close.

**Train:** Academy course "Land navigation".

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on estimating range and time, paraphrased.
