1. Say: you are safe, this will pass, it is not dangerous. Stay with the person.
2. Slow the breathing together: in through the nose for 4, hold for 2, out through the mouth for 6.
3. Ground them: name 5 things they can see, 4 they can touch, 3 they can hear.
4. Move to a quieter place. Do not crowd the person.

**Call 911** if it could be a heart attack (chest pain, pain in the arm or jaw, sweating, shortness of breath, first time), or if the person has thoughts of harming themselves. In the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

## If no help is coming

- **Most attacks peak in about 10 minutes** and pass within half an hour. Stay and keep your voice slow and low.
- **Give a simple job:** hold this, count that. It breaks the loop.
- **If chest pain is new, crushing or spreading,** treat it as a heart attack. See "Heart attack".
- **Afterwards:** water, food, rest, and a quiet talk. Say what helped, so next time is easier.
- **If the person talks of suicide,** see "Someone is thinking of suicide".

## Know more

### What it is
A surge of fear with pounding heart, shaking, dizziness and a feeling of unreality. It feels like dying but is not dangerous.

### Fast breathing
Over-breathing makes the tingling and dizziness worse. Slow breathing fixes it.

### In a group
One calm person helps more than advice. Keep others from crowding.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".

Source: National Institute of Mental Health and American Psychological Association guidance.
