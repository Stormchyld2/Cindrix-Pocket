**Natural warnings:** a strong or long earthquake, a loud roar from the ocean, or the sea pulling far back. **Do not wait for a siren.**
1. **Go to high ground or inland right away,** on foot if possible. Local signs often say at least 100 feet above sea level or 2 miles inland.
2. Take only your go-bag. Help those who cannot move fast.
3. The first wave may not be the largest. Go as high and as far as you can.
4. **If phones work, text, do not call.** Call 911 only for life-threatening injuries.

## If no help is coming

- **Stay away from the shore** until officials say all is clear. That can take many hours or a day.
- **Last choice:** the upper floor of a tall, strong building, or a tree. Better than the sea.
- **If caught in the water:** grab something that floats, keep your head up, and move away from debris.
- **After:** treat for cold water and drowning. See "Drowning or water rescue" and "Hypothermia and frostbite".
- **Water and food:** salt water has contaminated wells. Treat all water. See "Making water safe: boil, filter, treat".
- **Plan before:** pick a high spot, walk the route, and note travel time. Know your zone.

## Know more

### Waves and time
A big quake near the coast gives only minutes. A far-off quake gives hours.

### Tsunami waves
Many waves over hours. The sea may drain away first. Never go to look.

**Train:** Academy course "Earthquake preparedness".

Source: Ready.gov and National Weather Service guidance.
