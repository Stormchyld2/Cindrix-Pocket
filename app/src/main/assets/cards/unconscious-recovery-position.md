1. **Check for danger, then tap and shout.** If there is no answer and phones work, call 911.
2. Look at the chest for up to 10 seconds. If the person is **not breathing normally**, start CPR (see the CPR card).
3. If breathing normally and no spine injury is suspected: kneel beside them. Place the near arm out at a right angle. Bring the far arm across the chest and hold the back of the hand against their cheek.
4. Bend the far knee up, then roll the person toward you onto their side. Tilt the head back a little to keep the airway open.
5. Stay with them. Check breathing often. Keep them warm.

**Do not** give anything by mouth, put a pillow under the head, or leave them on their back. If a neck or back injury is likely, move them as little as possible (see the card "Neck or back injury").

## If no help is coming

- **Look for the cause** while you watch the breathing: a blow to the head, low blood sugar in a diabetic, heat, cold, poison or drugs, a seizure, a stroke, bad infection, heavy blood loss. Check for a medical bracelet or card.
- **Check every 15 minutes:** breathing, pulse, skin color, and whether they react to your voice or a pinch. Write down the time and what you find, so you can tell if they are getting better or worse.
- **Turn them to the other side every 2 hours** to prevent pressure sores. Pad bony places.
- **Nothing by mouth** until they are awake enough to sit up and swallow on purpose. Wet the lips with a damp cloth.
- **Keep them clean and dry.** They will pass urine. Change wet clothes and bedding.
- More than a few hours without waking is very serious. Carry them to help if there is any help to reach.

## Know more

### How awake are they
Use four plain levels and write down which one: **Alert**; answers to **Voice**; reacts only to **Pain** (a firm pinch of the shoulder muscle); **Unresponsive**. Moving down the list is the clearest sign of getting worse.

### Low blood sugar
A diabetic who is drowsy but can still swallow needs sugar now. One who cannot swallow gets nothing by mouth: see the card "Low blood sugar".

### After a seizure
Sleepiness and confusion for up to an hour are normal. Keep them on their side and let them wake in their own time. See the card "Seizure".

### A pregnant woman
Lay her on her **left** side.

### When they wake
Expect confusion. Tell them where they are and what happened. Sit them up slowly, give small sips first, and do not let them walk alone for the first hours.

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".
