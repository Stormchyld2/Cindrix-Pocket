**Say it plainly. FRS is a shared channel and anyone can hear it. If you can reach 911 and someone is in danger, call.**

**Red** is danger to life. **Amber** is a significant problem: failed radio, low power, a late return. **Green** is a routine "task done".

1. **Listen first,** then press and pause before you speak.
2. **Say "Emergency, emergency."** Then who you call and who you are: "Base, this is Patrol One."
3. **Where you are. What is wrong. How many people. What you need.** End with "Over."
4. **Base repeats the important details back** and sends help. Everyone else keeps the channel clear.

## If no help is coming

- **Example:** "Emergency, emergency. Base, this is Patrol One. Medical emergency near the south trail entrance. One injured adult, conscious and breathing. Request first-aid help. Over."
- **Order of traffic:** danger to life, then urgent medical or safety, then lost contact or someone overdue, then weather, then routine, then everything else.
- **No answer:** repeat once, move to higher ground, and try the back-up channel. See "Moving to the back-up radio channel".
- **Still none:** go to the plan. See "Base radio failed or lost contact with a patrol".
- **After the danger is handled,** write it in the log.

## Know more

### Why order matters
Clear, short calls get help sooner and keep the channel free.

### Use plain words
No codes that others will not know.

**Train:** Academy course "Communications".

Source: Cindrix Communications and Watch Operations Plan (COPS-COM-001, section 8), the family's own draft standard. Check it against your actual radios before relying on it.

Reference only, not medical or legal advice. Where help can be reached, call 911.
