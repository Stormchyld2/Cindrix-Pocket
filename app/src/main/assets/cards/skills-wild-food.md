**You can survive about 3 weeks without food. Water and shelter come first.** Wild plants are a risk when you are weak.
1. **Do not eat a plant you cannot identify with 100 percent certainty.** A few mouthfuls of some can kill. Look-alikes are common.
2. **Skip mushrooms** completely unless you are expert.
3. **Safer sources:** fish (cooked after cleaning), bird eggs, insects such as grasshoppers and crickets (wings and legs off, cooked), and plants you know well, such as dandelion, cattail, and berries you can positively identify.
4. **Cook animal food well** to kill germs and parasites.
5. **Do not eat** anything with milky sap, a bitter or soapy taste, white or red berries, or a bean-like pod you do not know.
6. **Learn now.** Take a local foraging class and use a field guide for your region.
7. **Conserve energy.**

## If you have less gear

- **No hook or net:** a spear or a trap, or look for shellfish and crayfish in streams.
- **No knife:** a sharp stone edge works.
- **Poisoning signs:** vomiting, cramps or confusion after eating. See "Poisoning or swallowed something".

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Eating on a hunch, eating mushrooms, and wasting energy on a long search.

### Practice
Learn five safe local plants well and find them in each season.

**Train:** Academy course "Food production and preservation".

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased; Poison Control and US Forest Service guidance.
