A map is only useful when it matches the ground.
1. **By compass:** put the compass flat on the map with the travel arrow pointing to the top. Turn map and compass together until the red needle sits in the orienting arrow (adjust for declination). North on the map now points to north on the ground.
2. **By features:** turn the map until a road, river or ridge lines up with the same thing in front of you.
3. **By the sun** with no compass: east in the morning, west in the evening.
4. **Hold it flat and keep your thumb on your position.**
5. **Look for catching features** ahead (a road, river or ridge) to tell you if you have gone too far.
6. **Check as you go.** At every feature, find it on the map and move your thumb.
7. **Fold the map** to the part you need and keep it in a waterproof bag.
8. **If you cannot match map to ground,** stop. See "Lost: stop and think (S.T.O.P.)".

## If you have less gear

- **No compass:** the sun and shadows. See "Finding direction without a compass".
- **No waterproof bag:** a clear plastic bag, or tape over a folded map.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Holding the map any way, and walking on when it does not match.

### Practice
Orient the map at every halt on a day walk.

**Train:** Academy course "Land navigation".

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on map orientation, paraphrased.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
