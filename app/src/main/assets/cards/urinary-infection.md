**Signs:** burning when passing urine, going often, a strong urge, cloudy or bad-smelling urine, low belly pain.

1. Drink plenty of water. Do not hold urine in.
2. A warm pad on the low belly may ease the pain.
3. A urinary infection usually needs antibiotics from a clinician. Do not use leftovers.

**If phones work, call 911 or get care soon** for fever, chills, back or side pain, nausea or vomiting, blood in the urine, or any symptoms in a man, a child or a pregnant woman.

## If no help is coming

- **Fluids, lots, all day.** It helps flush the bladder. Urinate often and fully.
- **Warning for the kidneys:** fever, shaking chills, flank pain and vomiting. This is a serious infection and can become sepsis. See "Sepsis: infection that is getting worse". Move toward care by any means.
- **Men, children and pregnant women** should be seen sooner. Do not wait.
- **Prevent it:** wipe front to back, pass urine after sex, keep the area clean, and do not hold urine.
- **Plan ahead:** a clinician may prescribe a course of antibiotic to keep for a long emergency.

## Know more

### Why it is common
Short tubes let germs reach the bladder, most of all in women.

### Dehydration makes it worse
Little water means concentrated urine and more germs.
