1. **Drink water** often, even if not thirsty. Avoid alcohol and sugary drinks.
2. Stay in the coolest place you have. **Do not rely on a fan** as your main cooling.
3. Cool showers or baths. Loose, light-colored clothing.
4. Hard work early or late, not at midday.
5. **Never leave a child, older person or pet in a parked car.**
6. Check on older neighbors, small children and people who are ill twice a day.
7. **If phones work, call 911** for heat stroke: confusion, hot dry skin, or fainting. See "Heat exhaustion and heat stroke".

## If no help is coming

- **Make a cool room:** the lowest, shaded, cross-ventilated space. Hang wet sheets in windows and open vents at night.
- **Cool a person fast:** wet the skin and fan them, cold packs on neck, armpits and groin.
- **Water plan:** more than you think. Add a pinch of salt to drinks if sweating a lot, and eat salty snacks.
- **Rest in the day,** work at dawn and dusk. Dig a shaded hollow if there is no building.
- **Watch the high-risk:** babies, older people, and anyone on regular medicine. Check twice a day.
- **Animals:** shade, cool water, and no walking on hot pavement.

## Know more

### Heat index
Humidity makes heat worse. Above 90°F with high humidity is dangerous for hours at a time.

### Nights matter
If the body cannot cool at night, risk builds day after day.

**Train:** Academy course "Power outage preparedness".
