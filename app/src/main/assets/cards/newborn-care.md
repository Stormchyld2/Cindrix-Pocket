1. **Keep the baby warm:** skin to skin on your chest, covered with a blanket and a hat. Newborns lose heat fast.
2. **Feed often:** at least 8 to 12 times a day. A baby that is feeding well has about 6 wet diapers a day after the first week.
3. **Always lay a baby on the back to sleep,** on a firm flat surface, with no pillows, loose blankets or toys.
4. Keep the cord stump clean and dry and fold the diaper below it. Wash hands before handling the baby.
5. Never shake a baby. If you feel overwhelmed, lay the baby down safely and step away for a few minutes.

**Get care now if it can be reached** (call 911 if phones work) for a temperature of 100.4 F (38 C) or higher in a baby under 3 months, trouble breathing, blue or gray skin, a baby who is hard to wake or will not feed, yellow skin that spreads, or fewer wet diapers.

## If no help is coming

- **Three things keep a newborn alive: warmth, milk and cleanliness.**
- **Warmth:** a baby that feels cool on the chest or has cold feet is too cold. Skin to skin under the mother's clothes, with a hat, day and night, is better than any wrapping.
- **Milk:** breast milk is the only safe food where water and fuel are short. Put the baby to the breast within the first hour and whenever it stirs, roots or sucks its fist. The first thick yellow milk is exactly what it needs.
- **Cleanliness:** wash hands before touching the baby, keep sick people away, and keep the cord dry.
- **Check every day:** feeding well, wet and dirty diapers, breathing easily, color, the cord, and how alert.
- **Danger signs:** not feeding, fast breathing (over 60 a minute) or the chest pulling in, grunting, fever or feeling cold, floppy or hard to wake, fits, yellow soles and palms, a red swollen smelly cord. A sick newborn gets worse in hours. Go to care if any can be reached.

## Know more

### Breastfeeding troubles
Milk comes in on day 3 to 5. Before that, frequent sucking is what brings it. The baby's mouth should take in a large part of the dark area, not just the nipple. A mother needs extra water, extra food and rest. Sore cracked nipples usually mean a shallow latch.

### No breast milk
Another nursing mother is the best substitute. Formula needs water that has boiled, clean bottles or a clean cup and spoon, and enough stored supply. Plain cow's or goat's milk, thinned grain water and sweetened water do not keep a young baby alive for long.

### Yellow skin
Mild yellowing of the face from day 2 to 4 that fades in a week or two is common. Feed often and give short spells of indirect daylight. Yellow in the first day, or spreading to the belly, arms, legs, palms and soles, or with a sleepy baby that will not feed, is dangerous.

### The cord
It dries and drops off in 1 to 3 weeks. Leave it alone and uncovered. Redness spreading onto the belly, pus or a smell is an infection.

### Diapers and skin
Change often, wash with clean water, let the skin dry in the air, and use petroleum jelly as a barrier.

### The mother
A mother who is exhausted, feverish, bleeding heavily or sinking into despair needs care too. See the card "Pregnancy warning signs".
