**Signs:** pale, blue or cold skin, tiny pupils, very sleepy or will not wake, limp body, gurgling or snoring sounds, slow or stopped breathing.

1. Try to wake the person: shake the shoulders and say their name loudly.
2. If you have naloxone (Narcan), **use it now:** spray into one nostril, following the instructions on the package. If phones work, call 911 or have someone call.
3. **If they are not breathing, give rescue breaths:** head tilted back, nose pinched, one breath every 5 seconds.
4. Turn the person onto their side (recovery position) once they breathe.
5. If there is no response in 2 to 3 minutes, give another naloxone in the other nostril.
6. **Stay with them.** Naloxone wears off and breathing can stop again.

**Do not** put them in a cold bath, make them vomit, or leave them to sleep it off.

## If no help is coming

- **Breathing for them is the treatment.** The drug stops the breathing, and the lack of air is what kills. With or without naloxone, rescue breaths keep the person alive until the drug wears off. Keep going, swapping rescuers, for as long as it takes.
- **Naloxone lasts 30 to 90 minutes. Many opioids last longer.** Watch the person without a break for at least 4 hours after the last dose, and longer for long-acting pills or patches. Have the next dose ready.
- **Take off any drug patch** from the skin and wipe the area.
- **When they wake** they may be sick, shaky, in pain and angry. That is withdrawal from the naloxone. It is miserable but not dangerous. Do not let them take more of the drug.
- **No pulse:** start full CPR. See the card "CPR: adult".

## Know more

### Who is at risk in a group
Anyone taking strong pain medicine, especially old people, anyone who takes more than prescribed when the pain is bad, a child who finds the pills, and a person who goes back to their old amount after a time without.

### Not only street drugs
Prescription pain pills, pain patches and some cough medicines are opioids. Mixing them with alcohol or sleeping pills is how most accidental overdoses happen.

### Coming off them when the supply ends
Stopping suddenly causes several days of aching, sweating, cramps, diarrhea, sleeplessness and misery. It feels terrible but is rarely dangerous in a healthy adult. Fluids, rest, and company. Reducing the amount slowly beforehand, with a doctor's plan, is far kinder.

### Have ready
Naloxone in the kit of any group where someone takes opioid pain medicine, a breathing mask, and all such medicines counted and locked.

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".
