1. **Run** if there is a safe way out. Leave your things. Help others if you can but do not wait. Keep your hands visible.
2. If you cannot run, **hide**: out of sight, door locked or blocked, lights off, phone silent. Stay low behind something solid.
3. **Fight only as a last resort** and only if your life is in danger: act together, use anything as a weapon, and commit.
4. **If phones work, call 911** when it is safe. Say where you are, how many attackers and what they look like or carry.
5. When police arrive: stay calm, drop what you hold, keep hands open and visible, and do what they say. Do not run at them.

## If no help is coming

- **No call will be answered,** so the plan is yours. Know two exits from every place you spend time.
- **Hide well:** lock, barricade with heavy furniture, silence all phones and radios, stay out of line of the door, and spread out.
- **Stop the bleeding:** a person hit needs a tourniquet or pressure at once. See "Severe bleeding".
- **Leave the area** only when you can see it is safe, and move away from the scene in a group and quietly.
- **Camp:** use the camp alert plan. See "Alert or noise at the camp edge" and "Someone has got inside".
- **After:** account for everyone and write it down. See "After an incident: account for everyone and write it down".

## Know more

### Run, hide, fight
Moving away is best, hiding second, and fighting only if cornered. Decide before you need it.

### Cover and concealment
Cover stops bullets (thick walls, engine blocks). Concealment only hides you (curtains, doors).

### Teaching children
Practice a quiet hiding game and one safe word.

**Train:** Academy course "Home and site security".

Source: U.S. Department of Homeland Security (Run, Hide, Fight).
