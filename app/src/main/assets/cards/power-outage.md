1. Use **flashlights, not candles.** Keep the **fridge and freezer closed.**
2. **Food safety (USDA):** a closed fridge keeps food cold about **4 hours.** A full freezer keeps about **48 hours** (24 if half full). Use fridge food first, then the freezer.
3. **Throw out** perishable food that has been above **40°F for more than 2 hours.** When in doubt, throw it out.
4. Keep phones charged on a power bank. Turn brightness down.
5. **Generators and fuel burners outdoors only.** See "Carbon monoxide" and "Using a generator safely".
6. Check on neighbors who need power for health. **If phones work, call 911** for a medical device failure.

## If no help is coming

- **Plan for days or weeks.** Water, food that needs no cooking, light, heat, and a radio.
- **Cooking:** outdoors on a camp stove or fire. Eat perishables first.
- **Heat:** one room, layers, blankets. See "Staying warm: C.O.L.D.".
- **Water:** if pumps fail, stored water first. See "Boil-water advisory and making water safe".
- **Medical devices and medicines:** plan backup power or a way to keep medicine cool. See "Diabetes when the power is out".
- **Light and power:** lanterns, solar chargers, batteries and a power station. Keep a stock.
- **Security:** a dark street changes the risk. See "Alert or noise at the camp edge".
- **Neighbors:** pool skills and tools. A group is safer than one house.

## Know more

### Food rules
A thermometer in the fridge is the best tool. Never taste to test.

### Sanitation
No power means no pumped water. See "Toilet when the water is off".

**Train:** Academy course "Power outage preparedness".

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service; Ready.gov.

Reference only, not medical advice. Where help can be reached, call 911.
