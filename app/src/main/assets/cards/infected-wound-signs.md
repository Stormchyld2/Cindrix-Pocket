1. **Look at every wound every day.** Signs of infection: redness that spreads, more pain after the first days, warmth, swelling, pus or a bad smell.
2. **Red streaks running up the limb, fever, chills, or feeling very sick** mean it is spreading.
3. Wash the wound again with clean water, remove dirty dressings, and cover with a clean one. Raise the area if it is swollen.
4. Get medical care early if it can be reached. Infections are far easier to treat early.

**It is an emergency** (call 911 if phones work) for a confused or very drowsy person, fast breathing, or a very fast heartbeat. See the card "Sepsis: infection that is getting worse".

**Do not** close a dirty wound tightly, or pour alcohol, iodine or peroxide into it again and again. These kill healing tissue.

## If no help is coming

- **Open it up.** If the wound was closed with tape or stitches, take them out so it can drain. Pus that cannot get out spreads inward.
- **Wash it out** twice a day with plenty of clean drinking water poured or squirted with some force. Wipe away pus and dead gray tissue with clean gauze.
- **Warm soaks:** hold a clean cloth wrung out in hot (not scalding) water on it for 20 minutes, 4 times a day. Heat brings blood to the area and draws the infection to a head.
- **Pack a deep wound loosely** with clean damp gauze so it heals from the bottom up, and change the packing at each wash.
- **Rest and raise the limb** above the heart. Keep the person fed and drinking.
- **Draw a pen line** around the edge of the redness and write the date. Redness moving past the line the next day means it is winning.
- **If the group holds antibiotics supplied by a doctor for wounds,** spreading redness, red streaks or fever is the time to use them as directed.
- **Getting better:** less pain, redness shrinking inside the line, less pus, pink bumpy tissue in the wound.

## Know more

### An abscess
A hot, swollen, tense lump with a soft center is a pocket of pus. Warm soaks bring it to a point. Once it is soft and pointing, it must drain: it often opens by itself after soaks. Then wash it out daily and keep it open until it stops draining.

### Normal healing looks like
A thin pink rim, a little clear or straw-colored fluid, and pain that lessens each day. That is not infection.

### High-risk wounds
Bites, punctures, crush injuries, wounds with dirt or wood in them, burns, and any wound on a diabetic or on the hands and feet.

### Lockjaw
Stiff jaw, trouble swallowing and muscle spasms days to weeks after a dirty wound. It is prevented by a tetanus shot kept up to date, which is worth doing now.

### Using honey
Plain honey spread on a clean dressing helps a shallow infected wound or burn. Change it daily.

### Have ready
Gauze, a large syringe or squeeze bottle for washing, clean stored water, a marker, soap, tape, and gloves.

**Train:** Academy course "Casualty care in a crisis".
