1. **Falling pressure and rising wind** often mean a storm is coming. A barometer or phone barometer helps.
2. **Dark, towering clouds** and a sudden temperature drop mean thunderstorms. See "Lightning".
3. **Wide gray, low cloud** and steady rain can last a day or more.
4. **A ring around the moon or sun,** with thin high clouds, often means rain within a day.
5. **Red sky at night** often means good weather ahead (works in many mid-latitude places, not everywhere).
6. **Local knowledge beats sayings.** Ask locals and watch.
7. **Use an official forecast** if you can: a battery or hand-crank NOAA weather radio gives warnings.
8. **Get off ridges and out of open ground** when a storm builds.

## If you have less gear

- **No radio or barometer:** watch the sky, the wind and the animals. Birds flying low and insects biting more often come before rain.
- **A notebook:** record the weather each day. Patterns show up in a week.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Ignoring the wind change, staying on a ridge, and camping in a dry streambed.

### Practice
Log the sky, wind and temperature each morning and evening for a month.

**Train:** Academy course "First 72 hours".

Source: National Weather Service weather safety guidance; US Army Ranger Handbook, paraphrased.
