Signs: cannot cry or cough, weak or silent, skin turning blue.

1. If phones work, have someone **call 911**. Do not wait for them.
2. Sit down. Lay the baby **face down along your forearm**, head lower than the body, holding the jaw. Rest your arm on your thigh.
3. Give **5 firm back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
4. Turn the baby **face up** along your other forearm, head still lower than the body. Give **5 chest thrusts** with two fingers in the center of the chest, about 1½ inches (4 cm) deep.
5. **Keep repeating** back blows and chest thrusts until the object comes out or the baby stops responding.
6. If the baby stops responding, lay them on a firm surface and **start baby CPR**. Look in the mouth before each breath and remove only what you can see.

**Do not** use belly thrusts on a baby, hold the baby upside down by the feet, or sweep a finger blindly in the mouth.

## If no help is coming

- **Keep going.** There is nothing more a rescue crew would do in the first minutes. Repeat the blows and thrusts without long pauses.
- **If the baby goes limp,** start CPR at once (see the card "CPR for a child or a baby"). Chest pushes may move the object.
- **After it is out,** hold the baby upright and calm. Let them feed when they want to.
- **Watch for 3 days:** a cough that stays, noisy or fast breathing, fever, or refusing to feed can mean a piece is still in the airway or lung.

## Know more

### A baby who is coughing or crying
A baby who can cry or cough hard is still moving air. Hold them and let them cough. Start back blows only when the cough goes weak or silent.

### Milk, mucus or vomit
For liquid, turn the baby face down along your arm with the head low and let it drain. Wipe the mouth. Back blows help here too.

### Stopping it before it happens
Babies put everything in the mouth. Keep coins, button batteries, beads, nuts and small toy parts out of reach. Anything that fits through a toilet-roll tube is too small. Cut food to pea size. Never prop a bottle.

### A button battery was swallowed
This is a different emergency: the battery burns the food pipe within hours. See the card "Swallowed object or button battery".

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".

Source: Red Cross (infant choking); American Academy of Pediatrics.
