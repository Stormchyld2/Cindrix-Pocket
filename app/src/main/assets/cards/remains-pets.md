**Wrap the pet, keep it cool, and bury it only high, dry and deep, well away from any water.** If someone is hurt or in danger, call 911.

1. **Wear gloves.** Wrap the pet in a towel or sheet, then a bag.
2. **Keep it cool** until you decide.
3. **If a vet put the pet down,** the drug can poison any animal that digs it up and eats it. Bury it deep and fenced, or use another method.
4. **Choose:** a vet, a pet cremation service, a pet cemetery, or burial on your own land.
5. **If you bury:** pick high, dry, deep soil, away from wells, springs, creeks, ditches and sinkholes, and from the garden. More than 2 feet of soil on top, more if dogs or coyotes dig.
6. **Mark the place,** wash your hands with soap and water, and write down where.

**Do not** bury a pet near water or on ground that floods or has sinkholes.

## If no help is coming

- **Cold storage** for a day or two. In heat, it does not last.
- **If it died of something catching,** or you do not know why, do not touch it bare-handed. Double bag it, keep other pets away, and use deep burial at a verified site.
- **Counties differ:** the Tennessee farm-animal rules cover livestock, not pets. Check your county.

## Know more

### Children
Tell them honestly and gently. Let them say goodbye if they want to.

### Have ready
Gloves, towels, bags, a shovel, a notebook.

**Train:** the plan "Remains management and water protection (SOP)".

Source: Tennessee guide sheet TN316 (cover and scavenger guidance); CDC and veterinary public guidance on handling dead animals.

Reference only, not veterinary or legal advice. Where help can be reached, call 911.
