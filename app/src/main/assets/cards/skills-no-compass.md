1. **The sun** rises roughly in the east and sets roughly in the west. At noon in the northern hemisphere, it is in the south.
2. **Shadow stick:** push a straight stick upright into level ground. Mark the tip of its shadow. Wait 15 minutes and mark the new tip. A line from the first mark through the second runs **west to east.** Stand with the first mark on your left and the second on your right, and you face north.
3. **Watch method** (northern hemisphere): point the hour hand at the sun. Halfway between the hour hand and 12 o'clock is south. (Use 1 o'clock in daylight saving time.)
4. **The night sky:** find the **North Star.** Find the Big Dipper and follow the two stars at the front of its bowl up, about five times their gap. The North Star is there, due north.
5. **Check with two methods.** Moss and tree growth are **not reliable.**
6. **If you know the area,** use roads, rivers, power lines and railways.

## If you have less gear

- **No watch:** the shadow stick works. Or use the stars.
- **Cloudy:** use the slope of the land, water flow and roads.
- **Make a compass:** rub a needle on silk or fur and float it on a leaf in still water. It will point north-south.

## Know more

### Common mistakes
Trusting moss, and using one method only.

### Practice
Use the shadow stick on a clear day and check it against a real compass.

**Train:** Academy course "Land navigation".

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased.
