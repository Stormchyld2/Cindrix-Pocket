Croup is a swollen windpipe in a small child: a barking cough like a seal, a hoarse voice, and noisy breathing in. It is usually worse at night.

1. **Stay calm.** Crying makes breathing harder. Hold and soothe the child.
2. Take them outside into **cool night air** for 10 to 15 minutes, or sit with them in a steamy room.
3. Keep the child upright and give small drinks.
4. Let them sleep propped up, with an adult in the room.

**It is an emergency** (call 911 if phones work) for noisy breathing while resting, drooling, trouble swallowing, blue or gray lips, a child struggling to breathe or too tired to cry.

**Do not** look down the throat with a spoon or put anything in the mouth, and do not give cough medicine.

## If no help is coming

- **Most croup passes in 3 to 5 days,** with the worst on the second or third night.
- **Keep the child calm above all.** A frightened, crying child breathes harder against a narrowed pipe. Hold them upright on your lap, speak quietly, and keep the room dim.
- **Cool, damp air** is the main help. Wrap the child up and sit outside, or by an open window. A wet cloth hung near (not on) the child helps in dry air.
- **Check through the night:** the noise on breathing in, the skin above the collarbones and between the ribs pulling in, color, and how alert.
- **Getting worse:** the noise present even when calm and asleep, deep pulling-in at the throat and ribs, restlessness then tiredness, going quiet and floppy. This needs a hospital at once if one can be reached.
- **Fever and pain:** children's fever reducer by the label.

## Know more

### Croup or something worse
Croup comes with a cold, a bark and a hoarse voice. A child who suddenly sits bolt upright, leans forward, drools, cannot swallow and has a high fever with no bark has a far more dangerous throat infection. Keep them upright, do not lay them down, do not look in the mouth, and go.

### Choking looks different
Sudden noisy breathing with no cold, in a child who was eating or playing with small things, is something breathed in. See the card "Choking: adult or child".

### Age
Mostly 6 months to 5 years. Older children get the same virus as a plain cold with a hoarse voice.
