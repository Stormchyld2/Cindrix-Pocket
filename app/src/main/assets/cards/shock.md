Shock means the body is not getting enough blood to its organs. Signs after an injury, bleeding, burn, bad infection or allergic reaction: pale, cold, clammy skin; fast, weak pulse; fast breathing; weak or faint; confused or restless; very thirsty.

1. If phones work, **call 911.**
2. **Stop any bleeding.** This comes before everything else.
3. Lay the person flat. If there is no head, neck, back or leg injury and it does not hurt, you may raise the legs about 12 inches.
4. Keep them warm with a coat or blanket under and over them, but not hot.
5. Turn them on their side if they vomit or get drowsy.
6. Stay and watch their breathing. Start CPR if they stop breathing.

**Do not** sit or stand them up, give alcohol, or leave them alone.

## If no help is coming

- **Treat the cause.** Shock does not get better until the cause is dealt with: stop the bleeding, cool and cover the burn, give the allergy injector, replace lost water.
- **Drinks:** if the person is fully awake, is not vomiting, and has no belly wound, give small sips of water or rehydration drink every few minutes. See the card "Dehydration and diarrhea" for how to mix it.
- **Check every 15 minutes** and write it down: how fast the pulse is, how fast they breathe, whether they answer sensibly, skin color and warmth, and whether they pass urine.
- **Getting better:** pulse slowing, skin warming and pinking up, clearer thinking, passing urine.
- **Getting worse:** pulse faster and weaker, breathing faster, more confused or sleepy, no urine for 6 hours. Carry them to help if there is any help to reach.

## Know more

### Why it happens
- **Blood loss,** seen or hidden inside. The most common cause after injury.
- **Water loss** from heavy diarrhea, vomiting, sweating or a large burn.
- **A severe allergic reaction.** See the card "Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)".
- **A bad infection** in the blood: fever or very low temperature, shaking, fast breathing. See the card "Sepsis: infection that is getting worse".
- **The heart** failing after a heart attack.
- **A spine injury:** the skin may be warm and the pulse slow. Keep them flat and still.

### Children and old people
A child can look well, then collapse fast. A fast pulse and cold hands and feet are the early signs. Old people and those on heart medicine may not get a fast pulse at all, so go by skin, thinking and urine.

### Fainting is not shock
A person who faints wakes within a minute once flat and feels better. In shock they stay pale, weak and thirsty.

### Keeping warm matters
A cold body cannot clot blood. Get the person off the ground onto a pad or blankets, replace wet clothes, and cover the head.
