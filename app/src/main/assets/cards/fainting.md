1. **Lay the person flat** on their back. Raise the legs about 12 inches (30 cm) if there is no injury.
2. Loosen tight clothing, belt or collar. Make sure they have fresh air.
3. Check that they are breathing. If not, start CPR, and call 911 if phones work.
4. If they wake within about a minute, keep them lying down for 10 to 15 minutes, then help them sit up slowly and sip water.
5. If they fell, check the head, neck and back before moving them.

**It is serious** (call 911 if phones work) if they do not wake in a minute, are not breathing normally, were hurt in the fall, have chest pain, a very fast or uneven heartbeat, trouble speaking or seeing, are pregnant, have diabetes, or faint more than once.

**Do not** sit or stand them up quickly, or give anything by mouth until they are fully awake.

## If no help is coming

- **A simple faint** has a clear trigger (heat, standing long, pain, fear, the sight of blood, getting up fast), a warning (dizzy, sweaty, sick, vision closing in), and a fast full recovery lying flat. Rest, water, salt and food are the treatment.
- **A faint with no warning,** during effort, while lying down, or with chest pain or a thumping heart is likely the heart. The person rests completely for several days and does no heavy work or work at height.
- **Look for a cause you can treat:** lack of water or food, heat, blood loss you have not found, low sugar in a diabetic, a new medicine.
- **Check pulse and alertness** every 15 minutes for the first hour.
- **Not waking after a few minutes** is not a faint. See the card "Unconscious but breathing: recovery position".

## Know more

### Stopping one that is coming on
Lie down at once with the legs up, or sit and put the head between the knees. Crossing the legs and tensing the thighs, buttocks and fists hard for 30 seconds can stop it.

### Why it happens
The brain is briefly short of blood. Heat widens the blood vessels, standing lets blood pool in the legs, and lack of water leaves less blood to go round. Fix those three and most fainting stops.

### Old people
Fainting on standing is common with blood pressure medicine and with not drinking enough. Get up in stages: sit for a minute, then stand holding on.

### A faint or a seizure
A few jerks while fainting are common and are not epilepsy. A seizure has longer jerking, often a bitten tongue or wet clothes, and a slow, confused recovery. See the card "Seizure".
