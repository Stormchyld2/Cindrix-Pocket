1. Stay calm and keep the person still. Do not poke with swabs, tweezers or fingers: it pushes the object deeper.
2. **Nose:** breathe through the mouth. If the object is loose and easy to see, a gentle blow out of the other nostril may move it.
3. **Ear:** tilt the head so the ear points down and shake gently.
4. **A button battery or magnet** is an emergency. If phones work, call 911.

## If no help is coming

- **Live insect in the ear:** tilt the head and float it out with warm (not hot) water or cooking oil, unless there is pain, drainage or a hurt eardrum.
- **Seeds and beans:** do not flush with water, they swell.
- **Parent's kiss for a child's nose:** close the clear nostril, give a firm puff of breath into the mouth. It often works.
- **If it will not come out,** stop. Keep the person calm, upright and breathing through the mouth, and get to care. An object left a day or two is usually not dangerous unless a battery or magnet.
- **After removal,** watch for pain, discharge or fever, and see "Earache or ear infection".

## Know more

### Why not dig
The eardrum and nose lining are easy to hurt, and digging pushes things further in.

### Children
They put things in their ears and noses. Keep beads, beans and small batteries out of reach.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".
