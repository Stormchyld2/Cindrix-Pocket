1. **Keep insulin cool but not frozen.** Unopened insulin keeps in the fridge until its date. Insulin in use can usually be kept at room temperature (below about 86°F / 30°C) for about 28 days; **check your own label**.
2. Without a fridge: keep insulin out of sun and heat. Do not let it touch ice or freeze. Throw out cloudy insulin that should be clear.
3. Keep a meter, strips, spare batteries and a paper log. Test as your doctor said.
4. Drink water. Eat on a regular schedule. Keep fast sugar (juice, glucose tablets, honey) within reach.
5. If you are sick, vomiting or have signs of ketones, call your doctor or 911 if phones work.

Insulin amounts are not given here. Follow your doctor's written plan.

## If no help is coming

- **Low sugar kills in minutes, high sugar in days.** When unsure which it is, and the person can swallow, give sugar. See the cards "Low blood sugar" and "High blood sugar and ketones".
- **Protect the supply.** Count every vial, pen, tablet and test strip, write down the dates, and use the oldest first.
- **Insulin past its date or kept warm** gets weaker, not poisonous. It is still far better than none. Expect to need to test more often.
- **Eat fewer starchy and sweet foods,** spread through the day, so less medicine is needed. Keep meals the same size and at the same times.
- **Stay active** with steady daily work. It lowers sugar. Carry fast sugar while working.
- **Illness raises sugar.** When sick, keep drinking water, keep testing, and never stop insulin because of not eating without a doctor's plan that says so.
- **Be honest about the limit.** A person whose body makes no insulin (type 1) cannot live without it. For them, the stored supply and any way to get more is the plan, and it has to be made before it is needed.

## Know more

### Keeping insulin cool without power
- **A clay pot cooler:** set a small clay pot inside a larger one with wet sand packed between them, a wet cloth over the top, in shade with a breeze. Keep the sand wet. Put the insulin inside in a sealed bag.
- A sealed jar lowered into a well, spring or cold stream, tied on a cord.
- A cellar or a hole dug two feet into shaded ground.
- Never the windowsill, a vehicle, or next to a heat source.

### Is the insulin still good
Clear insulin must stay clear, with no clumps, frost or color. Cloudy insulin types should mix evenly when rolled gently. If sugar readings climb with the same food and amount, the vial has weakened: open a fresh one.

### Testing without many strips
Test at the times that tell you most: on waking, and whenever the person feels unwell. Learn the person's own warning signs for low and high sugar and write them down so everyone knows them.

### Type 2 on tablets
Many people with type 2 can lower their need for medicine over weeks with less starch and sugar, steady daily activity and losing weight. Do not stop tablets suddenly: stretch the plan with the doctor beforehand if you can.

### Feet
Check the feet every evening for cuts, blisters and red spots. A diabetic's small foot wound can become a lost foot. Clean, dry socks and shoes that fit.

### Have ready
As much reserve insulin and tablets as a doctor will prescribe, rotated. Spare meter, strips, batteries, syringes, glucose tablets, ketone strips, and the doctor's written sick-day plan.
