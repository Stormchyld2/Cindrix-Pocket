**Belly pain can be a sign of something serious, such as appendicitis, a blocked bowel or a bleed.**

1. Have the person lie down in the position that hurts least.
2. Note where it hurts, when it started, any fever, vomiting, or blood in vomit or stool. Ask a woman of childbearing age whether she could be pregnant.
3. Give nothing to eat. Small sips of water only.

**Get emergency care now if it can be reached** (call 911 if phones work) for sudden severe pain, pain with fever, a hard stiff belly, vomiting blood, black tarry or bloody stool, chest or shoulder pain, trouble breathing, pain after an injury, pain in a pregnant woman, or pain that keeps getting worse.

**Do not** give laxatives or an enema for severe pain, or press hot packs on a belly that may have appendicitis.

## If no help is coming

- **Sort it by how it behaves.** Cramping that comes and goes with diarrhea or wind and no fever is usually the gut and settles in a day or two. Steady pain that gets worse over hours, hurts more with every movement or cough, and makes the person lie very still is the dangerous kind.
- **Press gently** in each quarter of the belly. A belly that is hard like a board, or pain that jumps when you let go quickly, means the lining is inflamed.
- **The dangerous kind:** complete rest, nothing by mouth except sips of water or rehydration drink, and keep them warm. If the group holds antibiotics supplied by a doctor for this, this is the time to use them as directed.
- **Check every 2 hours:** temperature, pulse, where the pain is, vomiting, whether they pass wind or stool.
- **Getting worse:** rising fever, faster pulse, belly swelling and going rigid, vomiting everything, no wind or stool, signs of shock.
- **What you cannot fix:** a burst appendix, a blocked bowel, a bleeding ulcer and a pregnancy outside the womb all need a surgeon. Some settle with rest and time. Go if there is anywhere to go.

## Know more

### Where it hurts
- **Starts around the navel, moves to the lower right over a day,** with loss of appetite and mild fever: appendicitis.
- **Upper right, under the ribs, after a fatty meal,** sometimes through to the right shoulder: gallbladder.
- **Upper middle, burning, better or worse with food:** stomach ulcer or acid. Black stool means it is bleeding.
- **One side of the back, coming in waves down to the groin,** with the person unable to keep still: kidney stone. Drink a great deal of water.
- **Low in the middle with burning urine:** bladder infection. See the card "Urinary infection signs".
- **Lower belly in a woman who may be pregnant,** with faintness or shoulder-tip pain: treat as an emergency.
- **All over, with a swollen belly, vomiting and no wind or stool:** blocked bowel.

### Children
Young children cannot say where it hurts. Worry about a child who lies still, will not jump or walk, has a fever, or has green vomit.

### After it settles
Clear fluids for a day, then plain food. See the cards "Vomiting", "Constipation" and "Dehydration and diarrhea".
