1. Rinse with warm salt water (a half teaspoon in a cup of warm water). Spit it out. Repeat a few times a day.
2. Floss gently to clear stuck food. Keep the head raised when lying down.
3. A cold pack on the outside of the cheek, 15 minutes at a time, eases pain and swelling.
4. For pain, use an over-the-counter pain reliever exactly as the label says. Do not put aspirin on the gum.

**Call 911** for swelling of the face, jaw or neck, trouble swallowing or breathing, or fever with a swollen face.

## If no help is coming

- **A swelling on the gum** is a sign of an abscess. It will not heal alone. Warm salt rinses can help it drain.
- **Spreading swelling, fever, or trouble opening the mouth** means the infection is spreading. This is an emergency. Move toward care by any means.
- **Do not poke or cut** the swelling yourself.
- **Antibiotics** may be needed. A clinician decides. Do not use leftovers.
- **A lost filling or cracked tooth:** a dab of clove oil or temporary filling material eases it until a dentist can help.
- **Keep the mouth clean:** brush gently around the tooth.

## Know more

### Why it hurts
Decay reaches the nerve. If the pain suddenly stops, the nerve may have died and infection may be growing.

### Prevention
Brush twice a day, limit sugar, and keep a dental kit in the supplies.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".

Source: American Dental Association and CDC oral health guidance.
