**Get inside. Stay inside. Stay tuned.**
1. **Get into the nearest building** and move away from windows. A basement or the center of a big building is best.
2. Close windows, doors and the fireplace damper. Turn off fans and air conditioning that pull in outside air.
3. Bring pets inside.
4. **Stay inside at least 24 hours** unless officials say to leave, or there is fire or a gas leak.
5. **Stay tuned** on a battery or hand-crank radio. If phones work, call 911 only for a life-threatening problem.

**If you were outside:** take off the outer layer of clothing (it removes most of the dust). Shower with soap and water or wipe skin and hair with a wet cloth. No hand sanitizer or wipes on skin.

## If no help is coming

- **Outdoors at the flash:** take cover and lie face down. After the shock wave passes, get inside. You usually have 10 minutes or more before fallout arrives.
- **Shelter quality:** more mass between you and the outside is better. Basement, center of the lowest floor, or a trench roofed with earth and boards.
- **Rule of thumb:** fallout drops about 90 percent after the first 7 hours and about 99 percent after about 2 days.
- **Leave only when the radio says so,** or fire or gas forces you. Then go quickly, in the direction officials give.
- **Food and water:** from inside a building is safe. Wipe sealed outdoor containers. Do not eat or drink what was open outside.
- **Sickness:** see "Radiation sickness (acute radiation syndrome)".
- **Keep a log** of who was where and when.

## Know more

### Dust, not rays, is the problem
Fallout is fine dust that lands and gives off radiation. Keeping it off skin and out of the house matters most.

### Potassium iodide
Take only if officials say. It protects only the thyroid.

**Train:** Academy course "Operator CBRN and hazmat protection".
