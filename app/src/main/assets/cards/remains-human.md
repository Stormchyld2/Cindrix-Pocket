**Keep the body cool and wrapped, away from food and water, and do not bury it until the site has been checked.** If phones work and the death was sudden, unexpected or violent, call 911.

1. **Make sure the person has died.** If they might only be unconscious, start CPR (see the CPR card).
2. **Wear gloves** and wash hands with soap after. Cover any cuts on your own hands.
3. **Photograph the face and note any marks or ID** before anything else. Write down date, time, place and who found them.
4. **Wrap in a clean sheet or bag and label it** with a waterproof tag. Keep it as cold as you can (best is 2 to 4 °C, 35 to 39 °F), shaded, apart from food, water and where people sleep.
5. **Tell the family and your group.** Allow a farewell and the customs the person would have wanted.
6. **Choose the arrangement** only after checking the site and the rules (see "Choose a burial or compost site").

**Do not** bury near a well, spring, creek, ditch or sinkhole. **Do not** use lime or chemicals: they do not help. **Do not** let children do the work.

## If no help is coming

- **Cold storage** buys time: a cold shed in winter, shade and ice in warm weather. In heat, bodies change within a day or two.
- **Burial:** only at a site that passes the soil, groundwater and karst checks. 5 to 10 feet deep, 3 to 4 feet of soil on top, the bottom well above the highest the water rises. Mark it, draw the map, write the record.
- **If they may have died of something catching** (cholera, a bleeding fever, a fast-spreading illness): untrained people should not handle these deaths; if you must, double gloves, gown or coveralls, boots, face shield, as few helpers as possible, and limit any gathering.
- **After:** wash up, change clothes, wash tools. Check on the people who did the work.
- **Keep records** for the family and for the land.

## Know more

### Are bodies a disease risk?
After injury, drowning or fire, bodies are not an epidemic risk by themselves. The risks are dirty water, bodies that died of an infection, and the person handling them.

### Tennessee
No state law forbids burial on your own land, but county zoning and health rules may apply, and all human remains are protected by law. A family burial ground recorded with the deed is protected. Check current law and your county.

### Have ready
Gloves, apron, boots, eye protection, sheeting or bags, labels, soap and water, shovels, a notebook, printed soil and geology maps.

**Train:** the plan "Remains management and water protection (SOP)".
