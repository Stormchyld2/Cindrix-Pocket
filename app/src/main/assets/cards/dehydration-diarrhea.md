Signs of dehydration: very thirsty, dry mouth, little or dark urine, dizzy, tired, sunken eyes. In babies: no wet diaper for 3 hours or more, no tears, a sunken soft spot.

1. Give **small sips often** of safe water or rehydration drink. If they vomit, wait 10 minutes and start again more slowly.
2. **Home mix (WHO):** 1 liter (about 1 quart) of clean, safe water + 6 level teaspoons of sugar + ½ level teaspoon of salt. Stir well. Make it fresh each day.
3. Keep eating plain food when able. Keep breast-feeding or feeding a baby.
4. Wash hands with soap after the toilet and before food. Use only safe water.
5. **Get medical care if it can be reached** for blood in the stool, a high fever, no urine for 8 hours, not able to keep fluids down, severe belly pain, confusion, or any baby or old person getting worse.

**Do not** give plain water only to a baby with diarrhea, or sugary soda or undiluted juice to anyone. Both make it worse.

## If no help is coming

- **Drinking is the whole treatment.** Most people who die of diarrhea die of water loss, and drinking prevents it.
- **How much:** after every loose stool, an adult drinks at least a large cup of rehydration drink, a child about half a cup, a baby a quarter cup, on top of normal drinking. Keep going until the urine is pale and plentiful.
- **Vomiting:** give one teaspoon every minute. Most of it stays down even when they keep being sick.
- **Too weak to hold a cup:** someone sits with them and spoons it in, all day and through the night.
- **Getting worse:** no urine for 8 hours, very sleepy or confused, cold hands and feet, fast weak pulse, skin that stays up in a fold when pinched. This is shock from water loss. See the card "Shock".
- **Find the cause:** bad water or food, or dirty hands. Treat all water and move the toilet area if more than one person falls ill.

## Know more

### No sugar or salt
Water that rice was boiled in with a pinch of salt, thin salted broth or soup, or weak tea with sugar all work. Alternate a salty drink with a sweet one.

### Food
Go back to food as soon as they will take it: rice, bread, potatoes, bananas, soup, cooked vegetables, eggs. Starving the gut slows recovery. Avoid greasy and very sweet food for a few days.

### Blood in the stool, or high fever
This is dysentery, an infection of the bowel wall that often needs antibiotics. Keep up the fluids and reach a doctor if there is any way.

### Stopping it spreading
The sick person uses a separate toilet place and their own cup and bowl. Everyone washes hands with soap. Wash soiled cloth away from the water source. See the card "Stop a contagious illness from spreading".

### Have ready
Packets of oral rehydration salts, plain sugar and salt, a measuring spoon, and a marked 1-liter bottle.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".

Source: CDC, WHO and Red Cross guidance on oral rehydration.
