Flu comes on **suddenly**: fever or chills, cough, sore throat, body aches, headache, tiredness. A cold comes on slowly with rare fever.

1. **Stay home** and away from others. Rest and drink plenty of fluids.
2. Cover coughs and sneezes. Wash your hands often.
3. **Antibiotics do not work on the flu.**
4. Go back to normal when you have been better for 24 hours and have had no fever without fever medicine.
5. Do not give aspirin to a child or teen with flu.

**If phones work, call 911 or get care now** for trouble breathing, chest or belly pain that does not go away, confusion, cannot wake up, seizures, no urine, or getting better and then much worse. For a child: fast or hard breathing, blue lips, ribs pulling in, not alert, or any fever in a baby under 12 weeks.

## If no help is coming

- **Isolate the sick person** in one room, with one carer if you can. Mask both for close care. See "Stop a contagious illness from spreading".
- **Fluids are the treatment.** Small sips often. Broth and boiled, cooled water with a little salt and sugar help replace losses.
- **Watch the lungs.** Fast breathing, fever that returns after improving, or a wet cough with chest pain may be pneumonia. See "Pneumonia warning signs".
- **Watch the high-risk people:** over 65, under 5, pregnant, or with asthma, diabetes or heart disease. Check them often.
- **Fever medicine:** follow the label for the person's age and weight. Never give aspirin to a child.
- **Recovery takes a week or more.** Fatigue lingers. Rest longer than you want to.

## Know more

### How it spreads
Droplets from coughing and talking, and hands touching faces. Most spread happens in the first 3 to 4 days.

### Cold or flu?
A sudden start with strong aches and fever points to flu. Slow start, runny nose and no fever points to a cold.

### Stopping spread in a group
One room, one carer, hand washing, clean shared surfaces, fresh air. Wash dishes in hot water and keep towels separate.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".
