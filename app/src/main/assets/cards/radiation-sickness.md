**Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned.** See "Nuclear blast or radiation". Do not leave shelter to look for care unless officials say it is safe, or a life is in danger.

1. **Wash off dust.** Take off outer clothing, bag it away from people, then wash skin and hair with soap and lukewarm water. Keep cuts covered.
2. Rest and give small sips of fluid. Write down when each sign started.
3. If phones work, call 911 for repeated vomiting that will not stop, seizures, confusion, trouble breathing, bleeding that will not stop, or severe burns or blisters.

## If no help is coming

- **Signs come in stages:** nausea and vomiting first, then a quiet time of hours to days, then the illness returns with fever, no appetite, diarrhea, and bleeding or infection in bad cases. Skin redness, blisters and hair loss follow high doses.
- **Care is supportive:** small sips of clean fluids, rest, a clean dry place, clean wounds and burns. See "Dehydration and diarrhea" and "Burns".
- **Protect the sick** from infection: keep them away from other sick people, wash hands, and cover wounds.
- **Dust, not the sick person, is the risk.** Someone who has washed off is no danger to others.
- **Potassium iodide (KI)** protects only the thyroid from radioactive iodine. Take it only if officials or a doctor say so, an FDA-approved product, following the package. Salt, iodized food and supplements are not substitutes.
- **Stay sheltered** as long as officials advise, usually at least 24 hours and often longer.

## Know more

### Who gets sick
Only those who get a very high whole-body dose in a short time, such as very close to a blast. Most people near an event will not.

### Contamination or exposure
Contamination is dust on you. Exposure is radiation passing through you. Washing fixes the first only.

### Recording
Keep a log of who was where and when, to help doctors later.
