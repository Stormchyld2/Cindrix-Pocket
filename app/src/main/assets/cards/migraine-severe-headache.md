1. Rest in a dark, quiet room. Put a cool cloth on the forehead or neck.
2. Drink water and eat something light if you can.
3. For pain, use the medicine your doctor told you to use, or an over-the-counter pain reliever exactly as the label says.
4. Write down when it started and what made it worse.

**Call 911** for the sudden worst headache of your life, a headache after a head injury, or with a stiff neck and fever, confusion, weakness, trouble speaking, vision loss, a seizure or fainting.

## If no help is coming

- **Warning signs mean act at once:** sudden thunderclap onset, headache with fever and stiff neck, headache with weakness or speech trouble, or after a blow to the head. These can be bleeding in the brain, meningitis or stroke. Keep the person still, head raised, and move toward care by any means. See "Stroke: BE FAST" and "Head injury or concussion".
- **A usual migraine:** dark room, fluids, rest, and a label-dose pain reliever. Hold off if vomiting, or if you have a stomach or kidney problem.
- **Caffeine** in a small amount sometimes helps, but too much brings rebound headaches.
- **Dehydration, hunger, poor sleep and eye strain** are common triggers in a long emergency. Fix those first.

## Know more

### Tension headache vs migraine
Tension is a band of pressure on both sides. Migraine is often one-sided, throbbing, with light or sound sensitivity, nausea, and sometimes an aura first.

### Triggers
Missed meals, little sleep, dehydration, stress, bright light, strong smells, alcohol, and withdrawal from caffeine.

### Keeping a log
Write the date, food, sleep and weather. Patterns show up in a few weeks.
