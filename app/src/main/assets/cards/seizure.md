1. Stay calm and **note the time**.
2. Clear hard or sharp things away. Cushion the head. Loosen anything tight around the neck.
3. When the jerking stops, roll the person onto their side.
4. Stay with them until they are fully awake. Speak gently and tell them what happened.

**Do not** hold the person down, and **put nothing in the mouth.** They cannot swallow their tongue.

**It is an emergency** (call 911 if phones work) if it lasts **more than 5 minutes**, another follows before they wake, they do not breathe normally afterward, they are hurt, in water, pregnant or diabetic, or it is their first seizure.

## If no help is coming

- **During the seizure** there is nothing to give. Protect the head, keep them on their side once you can, and keep the airway clear.
- **Afterward** expect up to an hour of sleepiness and confusion, a headache, sore muscles and sometimes a bitten tongue or wet clothes. Let them sleep on their side with someone nearby.
- **Look for a cause you can treat:** low blood sugar in a diabetic (see the card "Low blood sugar"), heat stroke (cool them), a high fever in a small child (cool them, see the card "Fever in a child or baby"), a head injury, poison, or lack of water.
- **A seizure that does not stop,** or one after another without waking, is life-threatening and needs medicine only a hospital has. Keep them on their side, keep the airway open, cool them if hot, and get them to help if any can be reached.
- **For the next day:** rest, food, water, and no climbing, swimming, fire-tending or watch duty alone.

## Know more

### A person with known epilepsy
Most seizures stop by themselves in 1 to 3 minutes and need only protection and rest. Missed medicine, lack of sleep, illness and alcohol bring them on. The person must never run out of or suddenly stop their medicine: stopping it can itself cause a seizure that will not end. Store a reserve and rotate it.

### Other kinds
Not every seizure is a fall with jerking. Some are a blank stare for seconds, lip-smacking, picking at clothes, or wandering confused. Guide the person away from danger and stay with them until it passes.

### A small child with fever
A seizure with a fever in a child between about 6 months and 5 years is frightening but usually harmless and lasts under 5 minutes. Lay them on their side, cool them, and treat the fever. A stiff neck, a rash that does not fade when pressed, or a child who does not wake properly afterward is a different and serious illness.

### Stopping alcohol or sedatives
A heavy drinker cut off from alcohol can have seizures 1 to 3 days later, with shaking, sweating and seeing things. Keep them safe, fed and drinking water, and never alone.

### Keeping them safe day to day
No bathing or swimming alone, no working at height, and a place away from the fire edge.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".
