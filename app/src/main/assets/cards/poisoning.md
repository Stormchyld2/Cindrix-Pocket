**If phones work: Poison Help line, free, 24 hours: 1-800-222-1222** (United States). Call 911 first if the person collapses, has a seizure, has trouble breathing or cannot be woken.

1. Get the person away from the poison, and into fresh air if it is a gas or fumes.
2. Find out **what, how much and when.** Keep the container.
3. **Skin:** take off contaminated clothing and rinse the skin with running water for 15 to 20 minutes.
4. **Eyes:** hold the eyelids open and rinse with clean lukewarm water for 15 to 20 minutes.
5. If they are drowsy, lay them on their side and watch the breathing.

**Do not make the person vomit.** Do not give salt water, oil or anything to "soak it up" unless the steps below say so.

## If no help is coming

- **Swallowed something that burns** (drain or oven cleaner, strong bleach, battery acid, lye): rinse the mouth, then give small sips of water or milk. Never cause vomiting: it burns again on the way up.
- **Swallowed fuel or solvent** (gasoline, kerosene, lamp oil, paint thinner): no vomiting, nothing to drink but a few sips of water. The danger is it getting into the lungs. Watch for coughing and fast breathing over the next day.
- **Swallowed pills, plants, mushrooms or bad food** within the last hour, and the person is fully awake: if you stock **activated charcoal**, give it mixed in water as its label says. It does not work for the two groups above, or for iron or alcohol.
- **Fumes or smoke:** fresh air, rest sitting up. For a fuel-burning stove, heater or generator indoors, think carbon monoxide: see the card "Carbon monoxide".
- **Check every 15 minutes** for the first hours: how awake, breathing, pulse, pupils, sickness.
- **Getting worse:** hard to wake, slow or noisy breathing, seizures, a very slow or racing pulse, yellow eyes after a day or two. Keep them on their side, be ready for CPR, and get to a hospital if one can be reached.

## Know more

### What it probably is
Pills and bottles nearby, burns around the mouth, a chemical smell on the breath, stained clothes, an open container, several people sick at once after the same meal or in the same room.

### Common dangers at home and camp
Medicines (especially heart, diabetes, sleep and pain medicines and iron tablets), fuel stored in drink bottles, cleaning chemicals, pesticides, antifreeze, wild mushrooms and berries, and carbon monoxide.

### Wild plants and mushrooms
Keep a piece of what was eaten. Sickness that starts 6 hours or more after eating mushrooms is the most dangerous kind, even if it seems to pass. See the card "Wild food: be very careful".

### Children
A single adult tablet of some medicines can kill a toddler. Keep all medicines and chemicals locked and high, in their own labeled containers, never in food or drink bottles.

### Have ready
Activated charcoal with its instructions, a list of every medicine in the camp and who takes it, and the poison section of your medical manuals marked.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".
