Signs: wheezing, coughing, tight chest, short of breath, cannot speak a full sentence.

1. Help the person **sit upright**, leaning a little forward with the arms resting on a table or the knees. Loosen tight clothing.
2. Help them use their **quick-relief inhaler exactly as their asthma plan says**.
3. Move away from smoke, dust, cold air or whatever set it off.
4. Stay with them and keep them calm. Fear makes breathing worse.
5. If phones work, **call 911** if there is no better breathing after the inhaler, or at once if they cannot speak, the lips or nails turn blue or gray, the skin between the ribs pulls in with each breath, or they are very drowsy.

**Do not** lay them flat or leave them alone. Medicine amounts are not given here.

## If no help is coming

- **Keep using the quick-relief inhaler** as the person's written plan says for an attack. Write down each time it is used.
- **Slow the breathing.** Breathe in through the nose for a count of 2, out through pursed lips, like blowing out a candle, for a count of 4. Do it with them.
- **A warm drink with caffeine,** such as strong coffee or tea, opens the airways a little for a few hours. It is weak help, but it is help.
- **Getting worse:** fewer words per breath, the wheeze going quiet while the struggle goes on, blue lips, exhaustion, drowsiness. A silent chest in a struggling person is the most dangerous sign.
- **Getting better:** longer sentences, slower breathing, the pulling-in between the ribs easing.
- **After an attack** the airways stay touchy for days. Rest, warm air, no smoke, no hard work, and the regular preventer medicine if they have it.

## Know more

### No inhaler at all
Upright position, pursed-lip breathing, a caffeine drink, calm, and clean warm air. Get them out of smoke and cold. That is all there is, which is why every person with asthma needs spare inhalers stored.

### Making an inhaler work better
A spacer gets far more medicine into the lungs. Without one, push the inhaler's mouthpiece through a hole in the bottom of a paper cup or a small plastic bottle and breathe from the open end.

### Common triggers at camp
Wood smoke, dust, mold in damp shelters, pollen, cold dry air, hard effort, and chest colds. Sleep the person upwind of the fire and give them a scarf over the mouth in cold air.

### Children
A child tires faster. Watch for nostrils flaring, the belly pumping, grunting, and being too breathless to drink or talk.

### Have ready
At least one spare quick-relief inhaler per person with asthma, a spacer, and a written plan from their doctor that says what to do in an attack.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".
