**Someone has a burn: cool it under cool running water for at least 10 minutes, and call 911 for a large, deep, face, hand or chemical burn.**
1. Get away from the heat or fire. Put out burning clothing: **stop, drop and roll**.
2. **Cool the burn** under cool (not ice-cold) running water for at least 10 minutes, up to 20. With little water, pour it slowly from a cup and catch it to reuse.
3. Take off rings and tight things near the burn before it swells. Do not pull off cloth that is stuck to the skin.
4. Cover loosely with a clean, non-fluffy dressing or clean plastic wrap.
5. **Chemical burn:** brush off dry powder, then flush with running water for 20 minutes or more. **Electrical burn:** do not touch the person until the power is off.

**Do not** use ice, butter, oil or toothpaste, and do not pop blisters.

**Serious burns** (call 911 if phones work): bigger than the person's palm with blisters, deep or white or charred, on the face, hands, feet, genitals or around a joint, from chemicals or electricity, smoke breathed in, or any burn on a baby or an old person.

## If no help is coming

- **Water is the danger with big burns.** Burned skin leaks fluid, and the person can go into shock over the first day. If they are awake and not vomiting, give rehydration drink in steady small amounts all day (see the card "Dehydration and diarrhea"). Plenty of pale urine means they are getting enough.
- **Keep them warm.** Cool the burn, not the person. A large burn makes people cold fast.
- **Raise** a burned arm or leg above the heart to keep swelling down.
- **Dress it once a day:** wash your hands, soak the old dressing off with clean water, rinse the burn gently, and cover with a clean non-stick dressing. A thin layer of honey or plain petroleum jelly under the dressing keeps it from sticking.
- **Watch for infection** from day 3: more pain, spreading redness, bad smell, green or cloudy fluid, fever. See the card "Signs a wound is infected".
- **Pain:** over-the-counter pain medicine by the label, and cover the burn, since air on a burn hurts.

## Know more

### How deep is it
- **Red and painful, no blisters** (like sunburn): heals in a week. Cool it and leave it.
- **Blisters, very painful, wet:** heals in 2 to 3 weeks if kept clean. Leave blisters whole. If one bursts, trim nothing, rinse and cover.
- **White, brown or black, leathery, little pain:** full depth. It will not heal by itself except from the edges, and needs a surgeon. Keep it clean and covered until then.

### How big is it
The person's own palm with the fingers is about one hundredth of their skin. More than about **10 palms** of blistered or deep burn in an adult, or **5 in a child**, is a danger to life from fluid loss.

### Burns of the face and airway
Soot around the nose or mouth, singed nose hairs, a hoarse voice or a cough after a fire means the airway is burned and may swell shut over hours. Sit them upright. You cannot fix this in the field: it is the strongest reason to get to a hospital.

### Hands, feet and joints
Dress each finger or toe separately so they do not heal stuck together. Start gently moving the joint through its range each day once the first pain settles, or it will stiffen for life.

### Have ready
Non-stick dressings, rolled gauze, clean plastic wrap, rehydration salts, and stored clean water kept just for washing wounds.

**Train:** Academy course "Casualty care in a crisis".
