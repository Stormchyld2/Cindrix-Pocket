Think of it after a fall from height, a vehicle crash, a diving accident or a hard blow to the head. Signs: neck or back pain, numbness or tingling, weakness, cannot move a limb.

1. **Tell them to stay still.** If phones work, call 911 and do not move the person.
2. Hold the head and neck still with your hands on both sides, in the position found.
3. Keep them warm.
4. **Move them only if there is a danger right there**, such as fire or rising water, and then drag them along the line of the body, keeping the head and neck in line.
5. Watch the breathing. Be ready to start CPR.

**Do not** twist or bend the neck or back, lift the head, or let them sit up to try it.

## If no help is coming

You cannot keep someone lying where they fell forever. First decide whether the spine is likely hurt.

- **The spine is probably not injured if ALL of these are true:** the person is fully alert and sober; they have no other injury so painful it drowns out everything else; they have no numbness, tingling or weakness anywhere; and when you press firmly on each bone down the middle of the neck and back, none of it hurts. If so, let them move slowly, stopping at any new pain.
- **If any one fails, treat the spine as injured.**
- **Hold the neck in line:** roll a towel or jacket into a thick collar around the neck, or pack rolled clothing on both sides of the head, and tape or tie the forehead to the surface.
- **Roll them as one piece.** One person holds the head and gives the count. Two more roll the shoulders and hips together, like a log, onto a board, door or stretcher padded with blankets.
- **Carry them flat** if you must move, with the head tied in line.
- **Care while lying flat:** roll them as one piece onto alternate sides every 2 hours and pad the tailbone and heels. Help with a container for urine. Keep them warm: they chill easily.
- **Getting worse:** numbness or weakness spreading, trouble breathing, cannot pass urine. Carry them to help if any can be reached.

## Know more

### Signs the cord is damaged
Cannot move or feel below a level, arms weaker than legs or the reverse, breathing only with the belly, skin warm and dry below the injury, a slow pulse with low energy. Some of this recovers over days as swelling goes down, so keep the spine in line and keep caring.

### Helmets
Leave a helmet on if the airway is clear and the head is held steady. Take it off only to reach the airway, with one person holding the neck from below while another eases the helmet off.

### Vomiting
Roll the whole body as one piece onto the side, head held in line, and clear the mouth.

### Back strain
Most back pain after lifting is muscle, not spine: no numbness, no weakness, and it eases with gentle walking, warmth and time.

**Train:** Academy course "Casualty care in a crisis".

Source: Red Cross first-aid guidance; Wilderness Medical Society (spine protection in remote settings).
