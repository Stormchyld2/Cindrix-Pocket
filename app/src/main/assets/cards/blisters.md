1. **Stop and deal with a hot spot before it blisters:** cover it with tape or a blister pad.
2. **Do not pop a blister** unless it is large, in the way of walking, and likely to burst. Whole skin is the best cover.
3. Cut a ring from padding (moleskin or foam) around the blister so the pressure is off it, then cover it.
4. Keep feet clean and dry. Change wet socks. Check shoes for rubbing spots.

**See a doctor if one can be reached** for spreading redness, warmth, pus, red streaks, fever, or a blister on a person with diabetes or poor feeling in the feet. In an emergency, call 911 if phones work.

## If no help is coming

- **Your feet are your transport.** A person who cannot walk has to be carried, so treat feet as seriously as any other injury.
- **Draining one that must be drained:** wash your hands and the blister. Heat a needle in a flame until it glows and let it cool. Make a small hole at the lower edge, press the fluid out, and leave the skin on as a cover. Dress it and re-drain if it refills.
- **A torn blister:** trim only the loose dead skin, wash, cover with a non-stick dressing, and pad around it.
- **Check every evening** by firelight or daylight. Wash, dry, air the feet, and change into dry socks for sleeping.
- **Getting worse:** cloudy or yellow fluid, redness spreading beyond the edge, heat, swelling. See the card "Signs a wound is infected".

## Know more

### Why they form
Rubbing plus damp plus heat. Boots too loose or too tight, wrinkled or wet socks, grit, and feet not yet hardened to the distance.

### Stopping them
Boots worn in before they are needed. Two pairs of socks, a thin one under a thicker one, so the rubbing happens between the socks. Toenails cut straight and short. Tape known trouble spots before setting out. Stop at the first warm rubbing feeling: five minutes then saves a day later.

### No blister pads
Smooth tape straight over a hot spot works. Duct tape will do. Put a small patch of cloth over an open blister first so the tape does not pull the skin off.

### Hands
The same care applies to blisters from tools. Gloves and a looser grip prevent them.
