**Dig sunchokes after frost, cook them well, and eat a small amount first.**
1. Find a tall, sunflower-like plant with rough leaves and small yellow flowers in late summer.
2. After the first frost, dig the knobby tubers.
3. Scrub them and cook them well (boil, roast or fry).
4. Eat small amounts at first, because they cause gas.

## If you have less gear

- **No spade:** use a stick or your hands in soft soil.

## Know more

### Look-alikes
Other sunflowers have no knobby tubers. Do not eat a root you cannot match to the plant above ground.

### When
October to March.

**Train:** The Academy course "Wild food of the Appalachians".

Source: WVU Extension and University of Tennessee Extension foraging guides; Peterson Field Guide to Edible Wild Plants
