1. Rest and drink plenty of fluids. Wear light clothing and keep the room comfortable.
2. A fever is the body fighting infection. You do not need to bring every fever down.
3. For comfort, you may use a fever reducer as the label says, if it is safe for you. Do not give aspirin to anyone under 19.
4. Check the temperature and write it down with the time and any other signs.
5. A lukewarm sponge bath can help.

**Get care if it can be reached** (call 911 if phones work) for a fever of 103 F (39.4 C) or higher, a stiff neck, severe headache, confusion, trouble breathing, a new rash, chest pain, repeated vomiting, fever for more than 3 days, or fever in a person with a weak immune system.

**Do not** use ice baths or alcohol rubs, or bundle the person up to "sweat it out".

## If no help is coming

- **The fever is the sign, not the illness. Find where the infection is,** because that decides what to do: cough and chest pain (lungs), burning urine or pain in the side of the back (kidneys), a red hot wound or skin, sore throat, earache, tooth, belly, or stiff neck and headache.
- **Drink far more than usual.** A fever uses up water. Aim for pale urine.
- **Cool for comfort:** light clothes, a damp cloth on the forehead, wrists and neck, a fan. Stop if they shiver.
- **Write down the temperature twice a day.** The pattern over days tells you more than one reading.
- **Getting worse:** confusion, fast breathing, a racing pulse, cold blotchy skin, no urine. See the card "Sepsis: infection that is getting worse".
- **Getting better:** fever lower each day, appetite returning, clearer head.
- A fever lasting more than 3 days with a clear source of infection is the usual reason to use antibiotics supplied by a doctor, as directed.

## Know more

### No thermometer
Lay the back of your hand on your own forehead and then theirs. A fast pulse, flushed face, shivering, and aching all over go with fever. Shaking chills followed by drenching sweats mean a high one.

### Fever with a stiff neck
Cannot touch chin to chest, bad headache, pain in light, and often a rash of small dark spots that do not fade when pressed under a glass. This is an infection around the brain. It is one of the few fevers that kills within a day. Go to a hospital if there is any way.

### Fever that comes and goes
Fever every day or two for weeks, with sweats: think of an abscess somewhere, a tick-borne illness, or tuberculosis. Look for a tick bite, a bad tooth, or a wound.

### Keeping others well
The sick person rests apart, with their own cup and bowl. See the card "Stop a contagious illness from spreading".

### Have ready
A thermometer, fever reducers, rehydration salts, and a notebook for recording temperature and signs.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".
