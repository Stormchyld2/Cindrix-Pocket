Use when a person does not respond and is not breathing, or is only gasping.

1. Check the scene is safe. Shout and tap the person.
2. If phones work, **call 911** or have someone call, and send someone for an AED (a defibrillator) if there is one.
3. Lay the person on their back on a firm, flat surface. Kneel beside them.
4. **Push hard and fast** in the center of the chest with both hands, one on top of the other, arms straight. Push **at least 2 inches (5 cm) deep**, **100 to 120 times a minute**, and let the chest come all the way back up each time.
5. If you are trained: after every 30 pushes, tilt the head back, lift the chin, pinch the nose and give **2 breaths**, each about 1 second, until the chest rises. Keep the pause under 10 seconds.
6. If you are not trained: keep pushing without stopping (hands-only CPR).
7. **Turn on the AED as soon as it arrives** and follow its spoken instructions.
8. Swap with another person every 2 minutes if you can.

**Do not** stop to check for a pulse again and again. Stop only if the person moves, breathes normally, or opens their eyes.

## If no help is coming

- **Be honest about the odds.** Without a defibrillator and a hospital, CPR alone rarely restarts a heart that stopped from heart disease. It is still right to try.
- **How long:** if there is no sign of life after **30 minutes** of good CPR, it is reasonable to stop. Stop sooner if the rescuers are worn out or the place is becoming dangerous.
- **Keep going longer** for cold water drowning, a very cold person, or a lightning strike. These people sometimes recover after a long time.
- **If they come back:** roll them onto their side (see the card "Unconscious but breathing: recovery position"), keep them warm, and stay with them. Breathing can stop again. Check it every few minutes for the first hour.
- **Afterwards** they need complete rest and, if there is any way, a hospital. Sore or cracked ribs are normal after real CPR.

## Know more

### Gasping is not breathing
Slow, noisy gasps or snoring breaths in a person who does not respond are a sign the heart has stopped. Start pushing.

### Drowning and choking
The problem is lack of air, so breaths matter most. Open the airway and give 2 breaths first, then 30 pushes and 2 breaths.

### Lightning and electric shock
Make sure the power is off or the storm risk has passed before you touch them. The heart often restarts by itself but breathing does not, so keep giving breaths even if you feel a pulse.

### A very cold person
A cold, stiff person may have a pulse too slow and faint to find. Check for a full minute. If there is none, start CPR and keep going while you warm them. See the card "Hypothermia and frostbite".

### A pregnant woman
Do CPR the same way. Have a second person push the belly gently toward her left side with both hands to take the weight off the big blood vessels.

### You hear or feel a crack
Ribs often crack. Check your hands are in the center of the chest and keep going.

### Have ready
A pocket breathing mask or face shield in each kit. A hands-on CPR class for every adult and teenager, refreshed every two years.

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".

Source: American Heart Association and Red Cross (adult CPR); Wilderness Medical Society (stopping CPR in remote settings).
