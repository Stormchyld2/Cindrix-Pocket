If phones work, **call 911** and stay on the line. Most births go well by themselves. Your job is to keep things clean, calm and warm.

1. Stay calm. Wash your hands. Lay down clean towels or cloth. Help the mother into the position she finds best, back propped up or on her side.
2. **Do not hold the baby in or pull on it.** Let the baby come. Support the head as it appears. If the cord is around the neck, slip it gently over the head.
3. Dry the baby and **place it on the mother's chest, skin to skin.** Cover both with a warm blanket and cover the baby's head.
4. If the baby does not cry or breathe, rub its back firmly and flick the feet. If still not breathing, give baby CPR (see the card "CPR for a child or a baby").
5. **Do not pull the cord.** The afterbirth comes by itself, usually within an hour.
6. Rub the mother's belly below the navel firmly to help stop bleeding.

## If no help is coming

- **The cord:** wait until it stops pulsing, a few minutes. Tie it tightly with clean string in two places, a hand's width and two hands' widths from the baby, and cut between the ties with scissors or a blade boiled for 10 minutes.
- **The afterbirth:** let it come. Never pull. Check it looks whole, like a flat round piece of liver, with no ragged missing part.
- **Bleeding:** a cupful or two is normal. For more, rub the womb through the belly wall hard until it feels firm like a grapefruit, put the baby to the breast, and have the mother pass urine. Keep rubbing every 15 minutes for 2 hours.
- **Heavy bleeding that will not stop,** or an afterbirth that has not come in an hour: this is the main killer of mothers. Keep rubbing, lay her flat with legs raised, keep her warm, and go to a hospital if there is one.
- **The first hours:** keep mother and baby together, warm, and feeding. Give the mother water and food.

## Know more

### The stages
Early labor: pains far apart, hours long. She can walk, drink and rest. Active labor: strong pains every 2 to 3 minutes. When she has to push, do not stop her. Between pains she rests and breathes.

### When to prepare
Clean cloths, boiled string and scissors, clean water, soap, a plastic sheet, blankets warmed by the fire, pads, and a light.

### Things that need a hospital
A foot, hand or bottom coming first, the cord coming before the baby, heavy bleeding before the birth, a fit, or pushing hard for 2 hours with no progress. If the cord comes first, have her kneel with her bottom up and chest down, and go.

### A tear
Small tears heal by themselves. Press a clean pad on a bleeding tear for 10 minutes. Keep it clean with boiled, cooled water after each toilet visit.

### The baby
See the card "Caring for a newborn". A baby that is pink, crying, and feeding within the hour is doing well.

### The mother in the next days
Bleeding like a heavy period that lessens over a week is normal. Fever, bad-smelling discharge, a hot painful breast, or a headache with swelling and blurred sight are danger signs. See the card "Pregnancy warning signs".
