1. Cut the line off the hook. Wash the area with soap and water.
2. If the barb is not buried, back the hook out the way it went in.
3. If the barb is under the skin, see "If no help is coming" below.
4. Clean the wound, cover it with a bandage, and watch for infection.

**Get a doctor** for a hook in the eye, face, a joint or a tendon, or a deep hook you cannot move. If phones work, call 911 for the eye or face.

## If no help is coming

- **Push-through method** (only for a hook in the fleshy part of a finger, arm, leg or back): push the point on through until the barb shows. Snip the barb off with clean cutters, then back the hook out the way the shank points.
- **String-pull method:** press down on the shank, loop strong line around the bend, and give a quick sharp pull along the shank line while holding the shank down. Warn the person first.
- **Never try either** near an eye, in the face, over a joint, tendon or large vessel, or if the hook is deep. Cover the eye with a rigid shield (a paper cup) and keep the person still.
- Wash well, dress, and check twice a day for infection. See the card "Signs a wound is infected".

## Know more

### Why the barb matters
A barb works like an arrow: pulling straight back tears the flesh. Pushing through or pulling along the bend frees it with less damage.

### Tetanus
Tetanus can follow any puncture. If the last shot was more than 10 years ago, see a clinic when you can.

### Fishing safely
Pinch barbs flat on hooks and wear glasses when casting or cutting line.
