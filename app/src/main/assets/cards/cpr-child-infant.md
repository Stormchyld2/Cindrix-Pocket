**Child** (about 1 year to puberty) or **baby** (under 1 year) who does not respond and is not breathing normally.

1. Shout and tap (for a baby, tap the foot). Check the breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
2. If phones work and someone else is there, one calls **911** and fetches an AED while the other starts. **If you are alone, give about 2 minutes of CPR first, then call.**
3. **Child:** push in the center of the chest with one or two hands, about **2 inches (5 cm)** deep. **Baby:** push with two fingers just below the nipple line, about **1½ inches (4 cm)** deep.
4. Push **100 to 120 times a minute**, letting the chest come all the way up.
5. After every 30 pushes give **2 gentle breaths**, each about 1 second, until the chest rises. For a baby, cover the mouth and nose with your mouth.
6. Use an AED with child pads if you have them. Keep going until the child moves or help takes over.

**Do not** skip the breaths. Children and babies mostly stop breathing for lack of air, so breaths matter more than in adults.

## If no help is coming

- **Keep going longer than you would for an adult.** A child's heart usually stops because breathing stopped, and good breaths can bring it back.
- If there is no sign of life after **30 minutes** of good CPR, it is reasonable to stop. Go on longer after cold water drowning or if the child is very cold.
- **If the child comes back:** lay them on their side, keep them warm, and do not leave them. Check breathing every few minutes for the first hour, then every 15 minutes through the night.
- **Find the cause** so it does not happen again: something in the airway, a bad chest infection, drowning, poison, a severe allergy.
- A child who needed CPR needs a hospital if there is any way to reach one.

## Know more

### Breaths that will not go in
Tilt the head again (a baby's head only slightly, to a level position) and try once more. If the chest still does not rise, something may be blocking the airway. Go on with chest pushes, and look in the mouth before each set of breaths. Take out only what you can see.

### Drowning
Give 2 breaths first, then 30 pushes and 2 breaths. Water in the lungs does not need draining. Do not waste time trying.

### Two people
With two trained people, give 15 pushes then 2 breaths, and swap every 2 minutes.

### How hard to blow
Only enough to see the chest rise. For a baby that is a small puff from your cheeks. Blowing too hard pushes air into the stomach.

### A very cold child
Check for a pulse and breathing for a full minute. If there is none, start CPR and warm the child as you go. See the card "Hypothermia and frostbite".

### Have ready
A child-size breathing mask in the family kit. A hands-on infant and child CPR class for everyone who looks after children.

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".

Source: American Heart Association and Red Cross (child and infant CPR); Wilderness Medical Society.
