A dislocated joint looks out of shape, cannot be moved, and hurts a great deal. Shoulders, fingers and kneecaps are the most common.

1. Keep the joint still in the position you find it. Support it with a sling, pillows or padding.
2. Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for 15 minutes at a time.
3. **Check the fingers or toes beyond it:** color, warmth, feeling, and a pulse if you can find one.
4. If phones work, call 911 or get to a hospital. A joint that has been out for hours is harder to put back.

**Do not** force the joint, and if a hospital can be reached, do not try to put it back yourself or give food or drink.

## If no help is coming

A joint left out stays painful and useless, and the blood supply can be cut off. With no help coming, it is reasonable to put back a **shoulder, a finger or a kneecap**, as early as possible, before the muscles tighten. Go slowly and gently. Stop if pain suddenly gets worse.

- **Shoulder:** lay the person face down on a table or log with the arm hanging straight down. Tie about 10 pounds of weight (a full water bottle bag) to the wrist. Have them relax. In 20 to 30 minutes the shoulder usually slips back with a clunk.
- **Finger:** hold the hand firmly, pull the finger steadily straight out along its line, and guide the base back into place. Tape it to the next finger.
- **Kneecap** (the cap sits to the outside of the knee): slowly straighten the leg while pushing the kneecap gently back toward the middle.
- **Afterward:** check color, warmth and feeling again. Rest the joint in a sling or splint for 2 to 3 weeks, then start gentle movement.
- **Do not attempt** an elbow, wrist, hip, ankle or the knee joint itself unless the limb beyond it is cold and blue with no pulse. Then pull gently in line, once, to restore the blood, and splint.

## Know more

### Dislocated, or broken
You often cannot tell, and both can happen together. If the limb is bent between joints, or you feel grinding, treat it as broken: see the card "Broken bone or bad sprain".

### Why speed matters
Within the first half hour the muscles are still loose and the joint goes back with little force. After a few hours they clamp down and it becomes very difficult without a hospital.

### After it is back
It will have torn the tissue that holds it, so it can come out again easily for some weeks. No lifting, throwing or reaching overhead with a shoulder for 6 weeks. Build strength back slowly.

### It will not go back
Splint it in the most comfortable position, give pain reliever by the label, keep checking the blood supply, and carry the person to help if any can be reached.

**Train:** Academy course "Casualty care in a crisis".

Source: American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons and Red Cross guidance; Wilderness Medical Society (dislocations in remote settings).

Reference only, not medical advice. Where help can be reached, call 911.
