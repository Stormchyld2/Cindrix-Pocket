1. **If someone is in danger now, call 911.** In the US you can also call or text 988, any time.
2. Stay with the person. Ask directly and calmly: "Are you thinking about killing yourself?" Asking does not plant the idea.
3. Listen without judging. Say you care and want to help.
4. Move firearms, pills and other dangerous items out of reach, if you can do so safely.
5. Do not leave them alone.

## If no help is coming

- **Stay until help takes over.** A trusted adult takes the next shift. Keep eyes on them, including at night.
- **Secure the means:** guns locked or removed, medicines and ropes put away, car keys held by someone else.
- **Talk about now and tomorrow.** Ask what is the worst part, and what would make tonight a little easier. Make a small plan for the next few hours.
- **Do not promise secrecy.** Say you care too much to keep this secret.
- **Check on them every day** for weeks. The days after a crisis are risky.
- **Look after yourself** afterward. Talk with someone you trust.

## Know more

### Warning signs
Talk of wanting to die or being a burden, giving things away, withdrawal, big mood change, reckless acts, and saying goodbye.

### What helps
Connection, a calm listener, and fewer ways to act on impulse. Most crises pass.

### Afterward
Arrange a doctor, counselor or the 988 line as soon as they can be reached.

**Train:** Academy course "Family medical care without a clinic".

Source: 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline and the National Institute of Mental Health.

Reference only, not medical advice. Where help can be reached, call 911.
