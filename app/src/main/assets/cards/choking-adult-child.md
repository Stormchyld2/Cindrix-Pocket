A choking person cannot cough, speak or breathe, may clutch the throat, and turns pale or blue.

1. Ask "Are you choking?" If they can cough or speak, **let them cough** and stay with them.
2. If they cannot, have someone call 911 if phones work. Stand behind, lean the person forward and give **5 hard back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. Then **5 abdominal thrusts**: make a fist just above the belly button, cover it with your other hand and pull sharply inward and up.
4. **Keep alternating 5 back blows and 5 thrusts** until the object comes out or the person stops responding.
5. If the person goes limp, lower them to the floor and **start CPR**. Each time before you give breaths, look in the mouth and take out anything you can see.

**Do not** slap the back of someone who is still coughing strongly, give them a drink, or sweep a finger blindly in the mouth.

## If no help is coming

- **Do not stop.** The steps above are everything a hospital crew would do first. Keep alternating blows and thrusts.
- **If they pass out,** the muscles relax and the object often shifts. Chest pushes during CPR can force it out. Look in the mouth every 30 pushes.
- **After the object is out,** sit them up and let them rest. A sore throat and a cough for a day are normal.
- **Watch for 3 days.** A cough that will not go, wheezing, fever or chest pain means a piece may have gone into the lung. Belly pain or vomiting after hard thrusts can mean an injury inside. Both need a doctor if one can be reached.

## Know more

### Pregnant or very large person
You cannot reach around the belly. Put your arms under the armpits and give the thrusts on the **center of the chest**, straight back.

### You are alone and choking
Lean over the back of a chair, a counter edge or a railing and drive your upper belly onto it hard, again and again. Do it before you get light-headed.

### A person in a wheelchair or lying down
Kneel astride the hips of a person on the floor, put the heel of one hand above the belly button with the other on top, and push in and up. For a wheelchair, lock the wheels and give thrusts from behind if you can reach.

### A child
For a child over 1 year, kneel behind so you are at their height and use less force. For a baby, see the card "Choking: baby under 1 year".

### Stopping it before it happens
Most choking is on food. Cut food small for children and old people, sit down to eat, and do not talk or laugh with a full mouth. Whole grapes, nuts, hard candy and chunks of meat are the usual causes.

**Train:** Academy course "CPR and First Aid".

Source: Red Cross (choking, back blows and abdominal thrusts); American Heart Association.
